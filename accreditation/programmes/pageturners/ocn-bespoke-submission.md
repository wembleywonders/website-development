## Submission covering note (for OCN London's curriculum team)

Wembley Wonders CIC is submitting seven gradable bespoke units plus one mandatory, ungraded
foundation unit covering its Pageturners programme — a creative-writing curriculum culminating
in the production of real, published in-canon fiction within WW's own "Easy Street" universe,
modelled on the tie-in/shared-universe fiction tradition (e.g. Black Library/Warhammer novels).
Unit 2 (Canon Literacy) is a mandatory prerequisite, assessed pass/fail, rather than a graded
unit — it establishes fluency in WW's own editorial rules rather than a transferable
creative-writing competency, and OCN London may wish to treat it as a non-credit-bearing
induction unit rather than a full unit within the qualification's credit total. All other units
are proposed at Level 2.

## Unit 1 — Creative Writing Fundamentals

**Level:** 2 | **Credit value:** 3 | **Guided Learning Hours:** 30

**Unit aim:** To introduce learners to the foundational craft elements of prose fiction writing.

**Learning outcome 1:** The learner will understand core elements of narrative craft.

- 1.1 The learner can define and give an example of point of view, voice, pacing, and dialogue in
  a piece of published fiction.
- 1.2 The learner can identify the difference between showing and telling in a sample passage.

**Learning outcome 2:** The learner will be able to produce a short piece of original prose
fiction.

- 2.1 The learner can write a piece of original short fiction (500–1,000 words) demonstrating at
  least two of the craft elements covered in outcome 1.
- 2.2 The learner can identify strengths and areas for development in their own work through
  self-review.

**Assessment method:** A submitted short-fiction piece with an accompanying self-review of
150–250 words.

## Unit 2 — Canon Literacy & the Walking Policy (Mandatory Foundation Unit — Ungraded)

**Level:** 2 | **Credit value:** 0 (pass/fail gateway, non-credit-bearing) | **Guided Learning
Hours:** 10

**Unit aim:** To ensure learners understand the shared-universe rules they must write within
before any graded creative work is undertaken.

**Learning outcome 1:** The learner will understand WW's Walking Policy governance for
shared-universe fiction.

- 1.1 The learner can explain, in their own words, the purpose of the Canon Lock,
  Non-Contradiction Test, First-Claim Rule, and Editorial Gate.
- 1.2 The learner can identify an example of a contribution that would fail the
  Non-Contradiction Test and explain why.

**Assessment method:** A short written or verbal check (pass/fail) confirming understanding of
all four governance mechanisms, administered before the learner proceeds to Unit 4 or later
units.

**Note for OCN London:** this unit is a prerequisite gate, not a certifying destination in WW's
own internal badge design — nobody progresses on knowing the rules alone. We are including it
here for completeness and transparency, but recommend it sit outside the qualification's credit
total.

## Unit 3 — Mythology & Heritage Research-to-Fiction

**Level:** 2 | **Credit value:** 3 | **Guided Learning Hours:** 30

**Unit aim:** To develop the learner's ability to turn researched heritage/mythological material
into original fiction.

**Learning outcome 1:** The learner will be able to identify usable source material for fiction.

- 1.1 The learner can select a piece of researched heritage or mythological material (their own
  or supplied) and identify its narrative potential.
- 1.2 The learner can distinguish between the factual/documented content of the source and the
  creative licence taken in adapting it.

**Learning outcome 2:** The learner will be able to produce original fiction adapted from
researched source material.

- 2.1 The learner can write a piece of original short fiction (500–1,000 words) adapting a named
  piece of researched heritage/mythological content.
- 2.2 The learner can explain, in a short accompanying note, which elements of the source were
  kept, changed, or invented, and why.

**Assessment method:** A submitted short-fiction piece with a source-adaptation note.

## Unit 4 — Character & World-Building Craft

**Level:** 2 | **Credit value:** 3 | **Guided Learning Hours:** 30

**Unit aim:** To develop the learner's ability to write coherently within an established
fictional world and its established characters.

**Learning outcome 1:** The learner will understand the requirements of writing within an
established canon.

- 1.1 The learner can describe at least two constraints of writing inside an established world
  (e.g. established character traits, established factional structures, established rules of the
  setting).
- 1.2 The learner can identify a research step needed before writing a scene involving an
  established character.

**Learning outcome 2:** The learner will be able to write a scene consistent with an established
world and its characters.

- 2.1 The learner can write a short scene (500–800 words) involving at least one established
  character, consistent with that character's documented traits and the world's established
  rules.
- 2.2 The learner can identify, in a short note, how their scene avoids contradicting existing
  canon.

**Assessment method:** A submitted scene with a consistency note, cross-checked against the canon
by the Keeper.

## Unit 5 — Genre & Tie-In Fiction Practice

**Level:** 2 | **Credit value:** 3 | **Guided Learning Hours:** 30

**Unit aim:** To introduce learners to the specific craft discipline of writing licensed
tie-in/shared-universe fiction, distinct from writing wholly original fiction.

**Learning outcome 1:** The learner will understand the tie-in fiction tradition.

- 1.1 The learner can describe what distinguishes tie-in fiction from original fiction, with
  reference to at least one real published example or tradition (e.g. Black Library/Warhammer
  tie-in novels).
- 1.2 The learner can explain why a tie-in writer has less creative latitude than an
  original-fiction writer, and where that latitude does still exist.

**Learning outcome 2:** The learner will be able to produce a piece of genre-consistent tie-in
fiction.

- 2.1 The learner can write a piece of short fiction (800–1,200 words) set within WW's
  established universe, consistent with its genre conventions and governance rules.
- 2.2 The learner can submit the piece for editorial review and respond constructively to at
  least one round of feedback.

**Assessment method:** A submitted tie-in fiction piece, reviewed and signed off by both the
Keeper (craft/writing quality) and Judith (editorial gate/canon compliance).

## Unit 6 — Editorial Process & Peer Review

**Level:** 2 | **Credit value:** 2 | **Guided Learning Hours:** 20

**Unit aim:** To develop the learner's ability to give and receive constructive editorial
feedback.

**Learning outcome 1:** The learner will understand the purpose and method of editorial review.

- 1.1 The learner can distinguish between developmental feedback (structure, character, pacing)
  and copy-editing feedback (grammar, consistency, style).
- 1.2 The learner can identify at least two techniques for giving feedback that is specific and
  actionable rather than vague.

**Learning outcome 2:** The learner will be able to apply editorial review to their own and
others' work.

- 2.1 The learner can provide written feedback on a peer's short-fiction submission, using at
  least one developmental and one copy-editing observation.
- 2.2 The learner can revise a piece of their own writing in response to feedback received, with
  changes identifiable between drafts.

**Assessment method:** A peer-feedback record plus a before/after draft pair showing revision in
response to feedback.

## Unit 7 — Vignette & Short-Form Writing

**Level:** 2 | **Credit value:** 2 | **Guided Learning Hours:** 20

**Unit aim:** To develop the learner's ability to write tightly-constrained short-form
biographical or narrative vignettes.

**Learning outcome 1:** The learner will understand the discipline of short-form vignette
writing.

- 1.1 The learner can explain what distinguishes a vignette from a full short story (e.g.
  compression, single focal moment, implication over exposition).
- 1.2 The learner can identify a real biographical source suitable for vignette adaptation (e.g.
  researched material from WW's own Roots programme).

**Learning outcome 2:** The learner will be able to produce a vignette from researched
biographical material.

- 2.1 The learner can write a vignette (200–400 words) based on real, verified biographical
  research.
- 2.2 The learner can identify which factual details were preserved and which narrative choices
  were added for effect.

**Assessment method:** A submitted vignette with a source note, distinguishing fact from
narrative embellishment.

---

## Unit 8 — Manuscript Development & Publication Practice (Capstone)

**Level:** 2 | **Credit value:** 4 | **Guided Learning Hours:** 40

**Unit aim:** To bring together all prior units into a real, published piece of in-canon fiction.

**Learning outcome 1:** The learner will be able to develop a manuscript from draft to
publication-ready standard.

- 1.1 The learner can produce a complete manuscript (scene, short story, or longer piece)
  consistent with WW's canon and governance rules.
- 1.2 The learner can incorporate at least one full round of editorial feedback into a revised
  draft.
- 1.3 The learner can prepare the manuscript for publication, including any required metadata or
  formatting for WW's Joystick portfolio platform.

**Learning outcome 2:** The learner will understand the publication process within WW.

- 2.1 The learner can describe the sign-off chain their manuscript passed through (Keeper craft
  review, Judith's editorial gate).
- 2.2 The learner can identify where their published piece sits within the wider Easy Street
  canon (e.g. season, faction, existing storyline it connects to).

**Assessment method:** A real, published manuscript on WW's platform, with sign-off records from
both the Keeper and Judith attached as evidence.

## Cross-programme note for OCN London's curriculum team

Unit 2 is the only ungraded unit in this set and functions as a gate, not a competency. All
graded units (1, 3–8) assess writing craft directly through submitted written work, which OCN
London's standard portfolio-based assessment approach should accommodate without modification.
Units 5 and 8 require dual internal sign-off (craft quality plus canon/editorial compliance) as a
condition of WW's own progression rules; OCN London's external quality assurance can be satisfied
by sighting the submitted manuscript and the internal sign-off record, without requiring direct
access to WW's full canon documentation.
