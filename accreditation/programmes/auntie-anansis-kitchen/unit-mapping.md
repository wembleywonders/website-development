### Unit Content Mapping (narrative modules → RQF units)

**AAK-1 — Caribbean Culinary Practice and Food Safety (RQF L2, 4 credits)**
Draws on: Module 1 (Food Safety, Hygiene and the Law), Module 2 (Caribbean
Culinary Foundations: Rice, Peas and the One-Pot), Module 3 (Spice, Heat and
Marinade: Curry Base and Jerk). Existing pathway coverage: Caribbean Cooking
Foundations (Perfect Rice and Peas, Curry Base Mastery, Jerk Seasoning) +
Food Business (Food Safety Basics). Cultural grounding carried in the unit,
not stripped for the RQF form: curry via Indian indentured labour; jerk as
Maroon preservation practice.

**AAK-2 — Culinary Heritage: Tradition, Documentation and Cultural Memory
(RQF L2, 4 credits)** Draws on: Module 4 (Regional Traditions and Island
Variation), Module 5 (Food as Calendar: Celebration, Ceremony and Grief),
Module 6 (Recipe Heritage: Oral Documentation and the Knowledge Commons).
Existing pathway coverage: the Recipe Heritage pathway (Documenting Family
Recipes, Understanding Island Variations, Food for Seasonal Celebrations).
Module 6 introduces the Knowledge Commons deposit pathway (consent,
attribution, protected-tier screening) and holds open — as live research,
not settled procedure — how figure-less communal practice (the Baianas do
acarajé case) is represented.

**AAK-3 — Food Enterprise: Costing, Trading and Market Practice (RQF L2, 3
credits)** Draws on: Module 7 (Costing, Pricing and the Food Business),
Module 8 (From Kitchen to Market: Trading, Scaling and the Capstone).
Existing pathway coverage: Food Business (Costing Your Dishes, Market Stall
Setup). AAK-3 is the capstone unit — a real, costed, compliant trading event
delivered end to end.

### External Qualification Anchor

Module 1 / AAK-1 aligns with the **Level 2 Award in Food Safety in Catering**
(RQF — the regulated qualification commonly called the "Level 2 Food Hygiene
Certificate"; ~9 GLH, 1 credit; awarded by Highfield, CIEH, HABC/NCFE and
others). A current certificate is accepted as evidence for AAK-1 criterion
1.1 (see `assessment-criteria.md`). This is the same kind of concrete
external-mapping anchor that TECHreneurs/STEMgeneers use against OCN/CVQ
business and STEM units.

### Apprenticeship Standard Mapping

**Production Chef (Level 2)** — strong mapping:
- AAK-1 covers: preparing, cooking and finishing dishes to a consistent
  standard; food safety, hygiene and allergen management
- AAK-3 covers (partial): controlling food cost, portion control and waste

**Hospitality Team Member (Level 2)** — partial mapping:
- AAK-1 covers: food and beverage service knowledge, safe working practices
- AAK-3 covers: customer-facing service and cash handling at a market pitch

Progression target: **Senior Production Chef (Level 3)** / **Hospitality
Supervisor (Level 3)** — AAK units provide the underpinning craft and
enterprise knowledge, not the full standard.

*Note:* AAK-2 (heritage documentation / oral history) has no close
apprenticeship-standard equivalent at Level 2 — its nearest external
reference is Oral History Society practice guidance and community-heritage
awarding-body units, not an apprenticeship.

### Skills for Jobs / T-Level Mapping

**Catering** T-Level — AAK-1 and AAK-3 provide foundation skills relevant to
the food preparation and culinary specialism. AAK-2 links to the **Craft and
Design** / cultural-heritage strand rather than Catering.

## Programme Rotation Credit

Completion of Auntie Anansi's Kitchen units contributes to:
- Knowledge Commons heritage-deposit strand (via AAK-2 / Module 6 — Esi's
  cross-programme link; a verified recipe-heritage record is a candidate
  KC deposit)
- TECHreneurs pricing rotation (via AAK-3 / Module 7 — a Kitchen product
  routed through the TECHreneurs pricing gate)
- ILP cultural-heritage strand and income strand
