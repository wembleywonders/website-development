# Auntie Anansi's Kitchen — Assessment Criteria

## Programme Overview
Auntie Anansi's Kitchen develops Caribbean and diaspora food heritage as
craft, cultural memory and livelihood — cooking the core repertoire to a
sellable standard, documenting family and community food knowledge before it
is lost, and running a compliant small food operation. Assessment is
competence-based — members demonstrate skills through real dishes, real
documented records and a real trading event, not examinations.

## Assessment Areas

### Unit AAK-1: Caribbean Culinary Practice and Food Safety (Level 2, 4 credits)

**Learning Outcomes:**
1. Apply food safety, hygiene and allergen law to preparing food for others
2. Cook the Caribbean one-pot repertoire to a consistent standard
3. Build and adapt a curry base and a jerk marinade from scratch
4. Explain the cultural history the techniques carry

**Assessment Criteria:**

| Criterion | Evidence Required | Pass Standard |
|-----------|-------------------|---------------|
| 1.1 Apply food safety and hygiene practice | Current Level 2 Award in Food Safety in Catering certificate, OR a completed food-safety knowledge check plus a witnessed prep session | Certificate in date; or all knowledge-check items correct and no critical hygiene fault observed (danger zone, cross-contamination, handwashing, allergen handling) |
| 1.2 Produce a Caribbean one-pot dish | Photo/video of the finished dish plus method notes | Rice cooked through and separate; seasoning balanced; technique (sealed steam, ratio) followed and described |
| 1.3 Build and adapt a curry base | Video or witnessed cook of a curry base taken to two proteins | Spices bloomed not burnt; base adapted correctly by protein; simmer time appropriate |
| 1.4 Make a jerk marinade from scratch | The marinade plus ingredient list and marinated result | Correct essential ingredients (scotch bonnet, pimento, thyme, scallion, ginger); wet paste consistency; scoring and marinating time correct for the protein |
| 1.5 Explain the cultural history of the techniques | Short written or recorded account | Accurately places curry (Indian indentured labour) and jerk (Maroon preservation practice), credited to the tradition of origin |

### Unit AAK-2: Culinary Heritage: Tradition, Documentation and Cultural Memory (Level 2, 4 credits)

**Learning Outcomes:**
1. Distinguish the regional traditions of the Caribbean and attribute dishes correctly
2. Explain how food marks the calendar, celebration and grief in diaspora life
3. Document a family or community recipe by oral method to a preservable standard
4. Apply the Knowledge Commons consent and attribution model to a heritage record

**Assessment Criteria:**

| Criterion | Evidence Required | Pass Standard |
|-----------|-------------------|---------------|
| 2.1 Compare regional traditions | Written or recorded comparison of at least three islands' traditions | Distinct histories and dish variations identified; dishes attributed to the specific island, not homogenised to "Caribbean" |
| 2.2 Account for food as calendar | Written or recorded account of at least three calendar/ceremony foods | Explains what each food marks and why; includes at least one ceremonial or funerary food (e.g. Nine Night) |
| 2.3 Document a recipe by oral method | Video of the cook-along, the written-up recipe with story, and the source's sign-off | Technique and observable cues captured (not only ingredients); "likkle bit" converted to measurement; recipe tested by the member; source has approved the written version |
| 2.4 Apply the Knowledge Commons consent model | Completed deposit consent record for the documented recipe | Deposit licence and the separate commercial-use opt-in both explicitly set; attribution recorded; protected-tier screening applied where a living non-consenting person is named |

### Unit AAK-3: Food Enterprise: Costing, Trading and Market Practice (Level 2, 3 credits)

**Learning Outcomes:**
1. Cost a dish accurately and price it for profit
2. Plan a compliant food-trading operation
3. Deliver and review a real trading event

**Assessment Criteria:**

| Criterion | Evidence Required | Pass Standard |
|-----------|-------------------|---------------|
| 3.1 Cost and price a dish | Costing sheet for at least one dish | Every ingredient costed per portion; hidden costs and a waste factor included; food-cost percentage calculated; selling price at or above the 3× floor with a stated rationale |
| 3.2 Plan a compliant trading operation | Trading plan: market choice, legal checklist, equipment list, menu with allergen matrix | Registration, insurance and hygiene certificate identified; hot-holding and transport covered; allergen matrix complete and correct |
| 3.3 Deliver a real trading event | Evidence of a completed market stall, supper club or pop-up (photos, sales record, temperature logs) | Event delivered; food held and transported safely (logs present); sales recorded |
| 3.4 Review the event | Written post-event review | Covers what sold, margin achieved against 3.1, and at least three specific changes for next time |

## Grading

All units assessed on Pass/Refer basis. No grades — competence is the standard.

**Referral:** Assessor provides written feedback. Member has 20 working days to resubmit.

**Appeal:** Follow standard appeals procedure (see quality-assurance/appeals-procedure.md).

## Assessment Methods

- **Portfolio of evidence** — primary method. Member compiles evidence in their ILP.
- **Observation** — assessor witnesses a live cook (AAK-1) and the trading event or a supper-club service (AAK-3).
- **Professional discussion** — assessor questions the member on food-safety judgement, the cultural history behind the techniques, and their costing and pricing decisions.

## Assessor Requirements

See quality-assurance/assessor-requirements.md. Subject requirement for this
programme: **Professional Cookery Level 3 (or equivalent industry
experience)** plus a current **Level 2 Award in Food Safety in Catering**.
AAK-2 documentation assessment additionally requires familiarity with oral-
history practice and the platform's Knowledge Commons consent model.

## Credit Recognition

Successful completion of all three units = 11 credits at Level 2. Maps to:
- Ofqual Level 2 Certificate equivalent (indicative — pending formal recognition)
- Wembley Wonders Auntie Anansi's Kitchen Builder badge
- ILP milestone: Culinary heritage practitioner competent
- Candidate Knowledge Commons deposit (from AAK-2 criterion 2.3, where the
  documented record is of wider research value and the member opts in)
