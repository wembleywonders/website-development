## Submission covering note (for OCN London's curriculum team)

Wembley Wonders CIC is submitting eight bespoke units covering its Roots programme — a
structured genealogical/archival-research curriculum for community members researching family
and diaspora history. Units 1–7 are designed to be taken individually or in sequence; Unit 8 is a
capstone requiring a real, citable output. All units are proposed at Level 2, reflecting an
adult, non-formal-education learner base with no assumed prior qualification. Assessment
throughout is portfolio/evidence-based rather than examination-based, consistent with OCN
London's standard bespoke assessment approach.

## Unit 1 — Archival Research Fundamentals

**Level:** 2 | **Credit value:** 3 | **Guided Learning Hours:** 30

**Unit aim:** To introduce learners to the practice of locating, accessing, and reading
primary-source archival material relevant to family and diaspora history.

**Learning outcome 1:** The learner will know how to identify relevant archival sources for a
given research question.

- 1.1 The learner can name at least three types of primary-source record used in
  family/diaspora history research (e.g. parish registers, shipping manifests, plantation
  records, census returns).
- 1.2 The learner can identify at least two real, accessible databases or repositories relevant
  to their own research question (e.g. the Trans-Atlantic Slave Trade Database, Jamaica Family
  Search, The National Archives).
- 1.3 The learner can explain the difference between a primary source and a secondary source.

**Learning outcome 2:** The learner will be able to conduct a structured archival search.

- 2.1 The learner can construct a search strategy for a named research question, including at
  least two named sources to be searched.
- 2.2 The learner can record search terms used and results obtained in a research log.
- 2.3 The learner can identify gaps or dead ends in the record and propose an alternative search
  strategy.

**Assessment method:** Research log plus a short written summary (300–500 words) of the search
strategy and findings for one real research question.

## Unit 2 — Record-Linkage & Verification Method

**Level:** 2 | **Credit value:** 3 | **Guided Learning Hours:** 30

**Unit aim:** To develop the learner's ability to connect records across sources and assess
whether they refer to the same person or event.

**Learning outcome 1:** The learner will understand the principles of record linkage.

- 1.1 The learner can explain what record linkage means in a genealogical/archival context.
- 1.2 The learner can list at least three pieces of corroborating evidence that would support
  linking two records (e.g. name, date, place, occupation, relationship).
- 1.3 The learner can describe at least one common cause of false linkage (e.g. common names,
  transcription error, place-name changes).

**Learning outcome 2:** The learner will be able to apply verification method to a real research
case.

- 2.1 The learner can present two or more records they believe refer to the same person/event,
  with the corroborating evidence identified.
- 2.2 The learner can identify the confidence level of their linkage (e.g. confirmed, probable,
  speculative) and justify this rating.
- 2.3 The learner can describe what further evidence would strengthen or overturn the linkage.

**Assessment method:** A worked case study submitted as a short written report, showing the
records used, the linkage reasoning, and the confidence rating assigned.

## Unit 3 — Oral History Interview Technique

**Level:** 2 | **Credit value:** 3 | **Guided Learning Hours:** 30

**Unit aim:** To equip learners with the skills and ethical grounding to conduct a respectful,
well-structured oral history interview.

**Learning outcome 1:** The learner will understand the ethical requirements of oral history
interviewing.

- 1.1 The learner can explain the purpose and content of informed consent in an interview
  context.
- 1.2 The learner can describe how to screen for and respond to a topic that may cause distress
  to the interviewee.
- 1.3 The learner can explain a subject's right to withdraw consent, including after the
  interview has taken place.

**Learning outcome 2:** The learner will be able to plan and conduct a structured interview.

- 2.1 The learner can prepare an interview question guide appropriate to a named research topic.
- 2.2 The learner can demonstrate active listening and appropriate follow-up questioning
  (assessed via recording or direct observation).
- 2.3 The learner can produce an accurate written or audio record of the interview with consent
  documentation attached.

**Assessment method:** A completed consent form, question guide, and interview record (audio or
transcript) for one real or simulated interview, reviewed by the Keeper custodian.

## Unit 4 — Evidence-Tiering & Citation Discipline

**Level:** 2 | **Credit value:** 2 | **Guided Learning Hours:** 20

**Unit aim:** To develop the learner's ability to grade the reliability of evidence and cite
sources consistently.

**Learning outcome 1:** The learner will understand evidence-tiering.

- 1.1 The learner can describe the difference between documented, contested, and
  original-research tiers of evidence, as used in WW's own Knowledge Commons.
- 1.2 The learner can give an example of a claim at each tier from their own research.

**Learning outcome 2:** The learner will be able to cite sources to a consistent standard.

- 2.1 The learner can correctly format a citation in the WW-Harvard style for at least three
  different source types (book, archival record, interview).
- 2.2 The learner can produce a reference list for a piece of their own research that is
  internally consistent and complete.

**Assessment method:** A short piece of research writing (500–800 words) with a full reference
list and each claim tagged by evidence tier.

## Unit 5 — DNA & Ancestry Literacy

**Level:** 2 | **Credit value:** 2 | **Guided Learning Hours:** 20

**Unit aim:** To give learners a basic, accurate understanding of DNA-based ancestry testing and
its limitations, distinct from STEMgeneers' genetics content.

**Learning outcome 1:** The learner will understand what DNA ancestry testing can and cannot tell
them.

- 1.1 The learner can explain, in plain terms, what an autosomal DNA test measures.
- 1.2 The learner can describe at least two limitations of commercial ancestry-testing services
  (e.g. reference-population bias, changing percentage estimates over time).
- 1.3 The learner can explain why a DNA match is not, on its own, proof of a specific
  genealogical relationship.

**Learning outcome 2:** The learner will be able to use DNA evidence responsibly alongside
documentary research.

- 2.1 The learner can describe how a DNA match could be used to support or challenge a
  documentary finding.
- 2.2 The learner can identify a privacy or consent consideration relevant to sharing DNA results
  with relatives.

**Assessment method:** A short written reflection (300–500 words) applying DNA literacy to a real
or hypothetical case in the learner's own research.

**Note:** this unit has no associated applied/practical destination and certifies Explorer-tier
comprehension only, consistent with WW's trigger-condition rule for content with no attached
material/technical tradition.

## Unit 6 — Material/Technical Tradition Identification

**Level:** 2 | **Credit value:** 2 | **Guided Learning Hours:** 20

**Unit aim:** To train learners to recognise when a research topic carries an associated material
or technical tradition, and to route it correctly.

**Learning outcome 1:** The learner will be able to identify material/technical traditions
attached to a research topic.

- 1.1 The learner can give at least two examples of a research topic with an attached
  material/technical tradition (e.g. Benin Bronzes → lost-wax casting) and at least two without
  one (e.g. migration records, naming-pattern traces).
- 1.2 The learner can apply WW's "does this topic have an associated material/technical
  tradition?" trigger question to a piece of their own research and justify the answer.

**Learning outcome 2:** The learner will understand the consequence of identifying such a
tradition.

- 2.1 The learner can explain what happens next for a topic that does carry a
  material/technical tradition (referral to Scrap Cat's Heritage Craft Revival pathway) before
  Builder-tier sign-off can be given.

**Assessment method:** A completed trigger-question worksheet for the learner's own research
topic, with written justification.

## Unit 7 — Provenance & Consent Ethics

**Level:** 2 | **Credit value:** 2 | **Guided Learning Hours:** 20

**Unit aim:** To ensure learners understand the ethical weight of researching living relatives
and family history that others have not consented to have made public.

**Learning outcome 1:** The learner will understand provenance and consent obligations specific
to family history research.

- 1.1 The learner can explain why family research carries a different consent standard from
  research into public historical figures.
- 1.2 The learner can identify at least two situations where a living relative's consent should
  be sought before publishing findings.
- 1.3 The learner can describe how to handle a request from a relative to withdraw or redact
  information.

**Assessment method:** A short written case study (300–500 words) describing how the learner
would handle a named consent scenario in their own research.

---

## Unit 8 — Knowledge Commons Deposit Practice (Capstone)

**Level:** 2 | **Credit value:** 4 | **Guided Learning Hours:** 40

**Unit aim:** To bring together all prior units into a single, real, citable contribution to
WW's Knowledge Commons.

**Learning outcome 1:** The learner will be able to produce a complete, evidence-tiered research
entry.

- 1.1 The learner can produce a research write-up incorporating archival sources, record linkage
  (where applicable), citation discipline, and evidence tiering.
- 1.2 The learner can identify whether their topic triggers the Unit 6 material/technical
  tradition pairing and, if so, evidence engagement with the paired Scrap Cat pathway.
- 1.3 The learner can submit their entry for Keeper sign-off, responding to at least one round
  of verification feedback.

**Assessment method:** A completed Knowledge Commons entry meeting WW's own publication
standard, with Keeper sign-off (and Maker co-sign where Unit 6 triggers).

## Cross-programme note for OCN London's curriculum team

Units 1–4 and 7–8 have no applied/practical counterpart and are assessed on research rigour
alone. Unit 5 is permanently Explorer-tier (no Builder-tier progression exists for this content,
by WW's own design). Unit 6 is the decision-point unit: where a "yes" answer is given, the
learner's Builder-tier progression in a real WW context requires crossing into a paired
practical pathway (Scrap Cat's Heritage Craft Revival) that sits outside this Roots unit set —
OCN London's assessment of Units 1–8 stands independently of that internal WW badge-progression
rule and does not require sight of the paired pathway's evidence.

## Internal verification notes (NOT part of the OCN submission — for WW's own record only)

Checked directly against this repo before filing, per this project's standing "provided ≠
verified" discipline, and against the established precedent from the Pageturners OCN submission
(file external-facing content as drafted, flag internally): the content above stays exactly as
supplied.

**Confirmed accurate, no issue:**
- `WW-SPEC-ROOTS-SYLLABUS-001.md` (this document's claimed source) genuinely exists at
  `docs/accreditation/`.
- **WW-Harvard citation style** is real and matches `src/knowledge-commons/citation/citationStore.ts`
  exactly (surname + year in-text, per `WW-SPEC-CITATION-001`).
- **Heritage Craft Revival / Maker+Keeper dual sign-off (Units 6, 8)** matches the existing
  `accreditation/programmes/roots/unit-mapping.md` closely — the same `material_tradition`
  trigger field, the same dual-sign structure (Keeper for research, Maker for craft
  identification), the same "Builder-tier and above only" gating. This is genuine, pre-existing
  WW accreditation design, not new for this submission.

**Minor, worth reconciling but not fabricated:**
- **Unit 4's "documented, contested, and original-research tiers... as used in WW's own Knowledge
  Commons"** slightly overstates the connection. The tiering language ("documented / contested /
  original") is genuine and matches the existing `accreditation/programmes/roots/evidence-requirements.md`
  and `assessment-criteria.md` closely — but it is not literally the live Knowledge Commons
  system's own vocabulary. The actual live `KcLiveEntry` schema (V71, backend) uses a different
  three-value confidence enum: `CORROBORATED` / `SINGLE_SOURCE` / `UNVERIFIED`. A third,
  independent vocabulary also exists in live frontend code:
  `src/pages/programmes/roots/RootsArchive.tsx`'s `EvidenceGrade` type
  (`documented | research | traditional | contested`). Three different tiering vocabularies now
  coexist across this codebase for conceptually the same question — worth reconciling at some
  point, not something this submission needs to resolve.

**The significant one — flagged prominently, not resolved here:**
- **This submission covers only the "archival-research strand" of Roots — and Roots, as a
  programme, currently has a second, unreconciled identity.** The real source spec
  (`WW-SPEC-ROOTS-SYLLABUS-001.md`) says so explicitly, in its own header warning: *"Roots
  already exists in the codebase as a body-sovereignty / hair-science / safeguarding resource,
  led by Judith Fontanelle"* — live, coded content at `RootsArchive.tsx`, `RootsPage.tsx`,
  `sandbox.tsx` (Hair Science by Texture, Chemical Literacy, a Remedies Database, an Apothecary
  workspace, the "Caring for Your Child's Hair" podcast). This OCN submission's eight units
  (archival research, record linkage, oral history, DNA literacy, citation, consent ethics) are
  an entirely different subject matter, hosted by a different lead (Esi/Keeper vs. Judith). The
  source spec's own words: *"Unresolved, flagged for CJ — not forced here: how these two strands
  ... sit under one programme badge, one host ROV, and one set of Explorer→Leader tiers... If
  CJ's intent was a single unified Roots syllabus covering both strands, stop and re-scope — this
  draft delivers only the archival strand."* That flag was never actioned before this OCN
  submission was drafted from it. Filing this as "WW-ROOTS" to an external accreditation body
  without disclosing that a member navigating to the live "Roots" programme today primarily
  encounters different content is a real gap — not a fabrication (everything in the eight units
  above is genuine, existing WW design for the archival strand specifically), but a scope
  question that should be resolved, or at minimum consciously accepted, before or during OCN
  review rather than discovered there.
