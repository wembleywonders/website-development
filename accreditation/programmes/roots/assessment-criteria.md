# Roots — Assessment Criteria

## Programme Overview

Roots (ancestral-record / archival-research strand) develops real ancestral and
community-history research — worked already braced for what the record cannot
give back. Members demonstrate skill through a real research log, real linked
records, a real oral-history interview conducted to consent standard, honestly
tiered citations, honestly written limitations, and a real Knowledge Commons
deposit. Assessment is competence-based — there are no examinations, and **a
piece of research that hits an honest structural wall and says so clearly is a
pass, not a fail.**

## Assessment Areas

### Unit ROOTS-1: Archival Research Discipline and the Structure of the Record (Level 2, 4 credits)

**Learning Outcomes:**
1. Explain why the archival record of enslaved and colonised people is
   structurally incomplete by design, not by accident
2. Plan and run a methodical archive search, keeping a full research log
3. Grade and cite evidence to the WW three-tier / WW-Harvard standard
4. Write a first honest "not found, and here is why" entry

**Assessment Criteria:**

| Criterion | Evidence Required | Pass Standard |
|-----------|-------------------|---------------|
| 1.1 Account for the structure of the gap | Short written or recorded account using the Antigua slave-register case (or an equivalent supplied case) | Correctly explains the record as a by-product of the owner's transactions; distinguishes a *structural* absence (no surname recorded because none was considered needed) from a search problem fixable with a better term |
| 1.2 Distinguish kinds of "cannot" | Completed Practice Exercise 1.1: one paragraph on a supplied record + family question | Correctly labels each limitation as not-indexed / not-in-this-record-set / structurally-never-recorded, with a one-line reason each |
| 1.3 Plan and log an archive search | Research log for a real first search (min. 6 sources consulted) | Every source recorded with what was searched and the result — **including nil returns**; name variants and transcription-error strategy shown |
| 1.4 Tier and cite evidence | Completed Practice Exercise 4.1: a supplied paragraph re-written with claims tiered and cited | Every factual claim carries a tier (documented / contested / original) and a WW-Harvard citation; unsupportable claims flagged as such, not cited |
| 1.5 Write a first gap entry | Completed Practice Exercise 6.1 at introductory scale (may use a supplied thread) | All six parts present (asked / searched / found / why the gap / what would close it / deposited as a finding); the *kind* of gap named |

### Unit ROOTS-2: Record-Linkage, Oral History and Honest Limitation (Level 2, 5 credits)

**Learning Outcomes:**
1. Link fragmentary records into a defensible lineage using James Dent Walker's
   method and the genealogical proof standard
2. Conduct an oral-history interview to the protected-tier consent and
   damage-screening standard
3. Read a DNA / ancestry result without over-reading it
4. Apply the consent ethics of research that touches living non-consenting
   people
5. Write an applied "Naming the Gap" entry for a real wall in your own research

**Assessment Criteria:**

| Criterion | Evidence Required | Pass Standard |
|-----------|-------------------|---------------|
| 2.1 Link records to a defensible standard | Completed Practice Exercise 2.1: a linked timeline from a supplied record bundle, plus one link-set from the member's own research | Each link graded (established / probable / possible / rejected) with a rationale; no link asserted on a single match; a "probable" link stated as probable, never firmed up |
| 2.2 Conduct an oral-history interview to standard | Recording of a 20–30 min interview **plus** the consent record and damage-screening note completed and dated **before** the interview | Consent covers recording, transcription, deposit and commercial use **separately**; damage-screening addresses the interviewee and named third parties; interviewee's right to review / redact / withdraw stated to them on the recording |
| 2.3 Interpret a DNA / ancestry result within its limits | Completed Practice Exercise 5.1: what a supplied result supports, suggests, and cannot address | Distinguishes a DNA match from a documented relationship; treats an ethnicity estimate as an estimate against a present-day panel; identifies at least one relative whose privacy the analysis implicates |
| 2.4 Apply consent ethics to non-consenting people | Completed Practice Exercise 7.1: a screening note for a supplied narrative with living named non-consenting people and a sensitive-but-true disclosure | Correctly separates the interviewee's consent from third-party consent; identifies who must be approached, what is held or redacted, what may be deposited now; a true-but-harmful disclosure handled as the subject's to make |
| 2.5 Write an applied gap entry | Completed Practice Exercise 6.1 on a real wall from the member's own 2.1 or 2.3 work | Six parts present; gap kind correctly named; entry reads as a deliberate finding, not an abandoned draft |
| 2.6 Spot a silent gap | Completed Practice Exercise 6.2 | Identifies the omitted unresolved question in a supplied "completed" entry and re-writes it as an explicit limitation |

### Unit ROOTS-2P: paired variant (Level 2, 6 credits) — *only when `material_tradition` = yes*

Everything in ROOTS-2 (criteria 2.1–2.6), **plus**:

| Criterion | Evidence Required | Pass Standard |
|-----------|-------------------|---------------|
| 2P.1 Identify the material / technical tradition | Written identification of the craft counterpart to the research topic | The tradition named specifically (not "a craft" but the technique, material and, where known, the community and line of transmission); attributed to its tradition of origin |
| 2P.2 Heritage-craft crossing | Evidence from the Scrap Cat Heritage Craft Revival crossing — a witnessed piece of craft practice **or**, where practice is not possible, a documented craft analysis | Meets the Scrap Cat criterion for the crossing (see `accreditation/programmes/scrap-cat/`); **dual-signed by the Maker (Scrap Cat) alongside the Keeper (Roots)** |

*Sign-off:* ROOTS-2P is not signed off by the Roots assessor alone. The Keeper
signs 2.1–2.6 and 2P.1; the Maker signs 2P.2. Both signatures are required for
the unit.

### Unit ROOTS-3: Knowledge Commons Deposit Practice — Capstone (Level 2, 3 credits)

**Learning Outcomes:**
1. Plan and deliver a substantial Knowledge Commons deposit end to end
2. State the standing of the research honestly — resolved, partial, or a wall
3. Connect the deposit into the wider Knowledge Commons

**Assessment Criteria:**

| Criterion | Evidence Required | Pass Standard |
|-----------|-------------------|---------------|
| 3.1 Deliver a substantial deposit | The deposited Knowledge Commons entry + the full research log (every source, including nil returns) + the consent / screening file | A real research question worked through the ROOTS-2 method; at least one oral-history interview where the question allows; fully evidence-tiered and cited; consent-screened |
| 3.2 State the research honestly | A short reflective account of where the research stands | Resolved, partially resolved or a wall — **stated as what it is**. If a wall: written in full Module 6 six-part form. **An honest wall is a full pass** — assessed on search rigour and honesty, not on whether the ancestor was found. A quietly incomplete entry (an unknown left unflagged) is a **refer**. |
| 3.3 Connect the deposit to the globe | The `cross_links` set on the entry, **or** a written sub-national-creation flag | Where a Knowledge Commons globe entry exists for the place: the deposit is cross-linked to it via `cross_links`. Where none exists: the place is flagged, in writing, as a candidate for demand-driven sub-national creation, addressed to Documenter / Archivist review — **not** self-created by the member |

## Grading

All units assessed on **Pass / Refer**. No grades — competence is the standard.

**On honest limitation:** a research finding that reaches a genuine wall and
documents it to the Module 6 standard is a **Pass**. It is not "incomplete". A
refer is for a search that was not reasonably exhaustive, a link asserted beyond
the evidence, a consent or screening step missing, or an unresolved question
left silent rather than named.

**Referral:** Assessor provides written feedback. Member has 20 working days to
resubmit.

**Appeal:** Follow standard appeals procedure (see
quality-assurance/appeals-procedure.md).

## Assessment Methods

- **Portfolio of evidence** — primary method. Member compiles the research log,
  linked records, interview recording + consent file, tiered citations, gap
  entries and the capstone deposit in their ILP.
- **Observation** — assessor witnesses at least part of one live oral-history
  interview setup and consent process (ROOTS-2, 2.2); for ROOTS-2P, the Maker
  observes the craft-practice element.
- **Professional discussion** — assessor questions the member on: why a given
  gap is structural rather than a search problem; why a link is graded where it
  is; the consent decisions made about non-consenting third parties; and, for
  the capstone, the honest standing of the research.

## Assessor Requirements

See quality-assurance/assessor-requirements.md. Subject requirement for this
programme: demonstrable practice in **archival or genealogical research** (a
records/archives or history qualification, or equivalent documented research
experience) **and** familiarity with **Oral History Society practice guidance**
and the platform's Knowledge Commons protected-tier consent model. ROOTS-2P
additionally requires a **Scrap Cat / Heritage Craft Revival assessor** as the
co-signer — the Roots assessor does not sign the craft criteria alone.

## Credit Recognition

Successful completion:

- ROOTS-1 + ROOTS-2 + ROOTS-3 = **12 credits** at Level 2 (unpaired route)
- ROOTS-1 + ROOTS-2P + ROOTS-3 = **13 credits** at Level 2 (paired route)

Maps to:
- Ofqual Level 2 Certificate equivalent (indicative — pending formal recognition)
- Wembley Wonders Roots Builder badge (ROOTS-1 + ROOTS-2/2P); Roots Leader badge
  (+ ROOTS-3)
- ILP milestone: Archival research practitioner competent
- Candidate Knowledge Commons deposit (from ROOTS-3 — the capstone deposit *is*
  a Knowledge Commons entry, whether resolved or an honest wall)
- Scrap Cat Heritage Craft Revival rotation credit (ROOTS-2P route only)
