## Programme Overview

STEMgeneers develops science, technology, engineering and mathematics skills
through real projects. Assessment is competence-based — members demonstrate
skill through worked problems, justified methods and independent verification,
not examinations. A member is assessed on the **method and the honesty of the
account**, not only on the final answer — a discipline this programme traces
back to Euclid.

## Assessment Areas

### Unit STEM-APM: Applied Mathematics & Physics — the History and the Verification Principle (Level 2, 4 credits)

**Learning Outcomes:**
1. Read and write within a number system other than everyday decimal, and
   state the contested readings of an early counting artefact honestly
2. Construct a simple mathematical proof as a justified chain of steps
3. Apply Archimedes' mechanics — the lever, buoyancy, and a bracketing
   (exhaustion) argument — to real problems
4. Express an equation-solving method as a general algorithm, and explain the
   shared root of "algebra" and "algorithm"
5. Differentiate and integrate simple polynomials and state what each
   operation produces
6. *(Builder tier)* Independently verify a real-stakes calculation and account
   for why the verification can be trusted
7. *(Builder tier)* Compare two ways a body of mathematical knowledge earns
   trust — lived practice and institutional rigour — without ranking one above
   the other

**Assessment Criteria:**

| Criterion | Tier | Evidence Required | Pass Standard |
|-----------|------|-------------------|---------------|
| 1.1 Work within a non-decimal number system | Explorer | Read and write 3 supplied values in a supplied non-decimal notation | All correct; the member states what makes the notation a *system* (a base + place value), not just a set of symbols |
| 1.2 State the Ishango bone's contested readings honestly | Explorer | One written "honest sentence" (or short paragraph) on what the Ishango bone is and what it shows | Names it as a real ~20,000-year-old artefact from Ishango, DR Congo; names **all three** disputed readings (tally / lunar calendar / intentional arithmetic); does **not** assert one as proven. Overclaiming ("proves early arithmetic") or underclaiming ("just a tally stick") is a refer. |
| 2.1 Construct a proof | Explorer | One simple geometric result proved as a numbered step-chain | Each step justified from a stated prior step or axiom; nothing assumed silently; the conclusion follows |
| 3.1 Apply the lever | Explorer | A solved real load problem using the lever ratio | Correct ratio, correct answer, working shown |
| 3.2 Apply buoyancy / displacement | Explorer | Volume of an irregular object found by displacement, or an upthrust calculated | Method correct (upthrust = weight of fluid displaced), answer reasonable |
| 3.3 Apply a bracketing argument | Explorer | π (or another value/area) bracketed between an under- and an over-estimate | Both bounds valid; the member explains that narrowing the gap is the idea calculus formalises |
| 4.1 Write an equation-solving algorithm | Explorer | A linear and a simple quadratic equation solved, each written as a numbered procedure | The procedure is general (would work on any equation of that form), not just the specific numbers; correct solutions |
| 4.2 Explain the algebra/algorithm root | Explorer | Short written or verbal account | Correctly attributes "algebra" to *al-jabr* and "algorithm" to Al-Khwarizmi's name; states why an equation-method and a program are the same kind of object |
| 5.1 Differentiate and integrate polynomials | Explorer | Worked set: differentiate and integrate simple polynomials; use a derivative to find a rate | Rules applied correctly; the member states in one sentence what integrating a rate gives back |
| 6.1 Independently verify a real-stakes calculation | Builder | A supplied calculation with a stated real-world stake, verified by an **independent method** (not by re-running the same steps), plus a short written account | The check uses a genuinely different route; the account states what would have to be true for the check itself to be trustworthy (the Katherine Johnson discipline) |
| 7.1 Compare two trust models | Builder | A written comparison of the Ifá (lived practice) and Katherine Johnson (institutional rigour under real stakes) trust models | Both described accurately and fairly; neither ranked as "the real one"; the member can say why the lived-practice model is also a legitimate way knowledge earns trust. *The Ifá cast-to-hex exercise itself is evidenced under its own spec and is not re-assessed here.* |

## Grading

All units assessed on **Pass / Refer**. No grades — competence is the standard.

**On honesty:** criterion 1.2 is a pass/refer on the *honesty of the claim*,
not on reaching a "right answer" about the Ishango bone — because there isn't
one. A member who names the dispute accurately passes; a member who resolves
it in either direction refers.

**Referral:** Assessor provides written feedback. Member has 20 working days
to resubmit.

**Appeal:** Follow standard appeals procedure (see
quality-assurance/appeals-procedure.md).

## Assessment Methods

- **Portfolio of evidence** — primary method. Member compiles worked-problem
  sets, the proof, the Ishango honest sentence, the verification write-up and
  the trust-model comparison in their ILP.
- **Observation** — assessor witnesses at least one worked calculation done
  live (guards against uncredited assistance on the problem sets).
- **Professional discussion** — assessor questions the member on: why the
  Ishango reading is contested and how they chose their wording; why a given
  proof step is justified; and what makes their independent verification (6.1)
  trustworthy.

## Assessor Requirements

See quality-assurance/assessor-requirements.md. Subject requirement for
`STEM-APM`: mathematics or physics teaching/practice to at least A-level or
equivalent applied experience, and familiarity with the history-of-mathematics
framing this unit uses (the unit is not a standard maths course — the
chronological spine and the verification-principle pairing are load-bearing,
not decoration).
