## Why CVQ leads here, not OCN London

OCN London's relevant provision (Industrial Textiles Skills, Level 1) is framed around
industrial/manufacturing textile production — specifications, quality control, safe processes —
not fashion design or garment construction. CVQ/NCTVET, by contrast, has real, named
occupational areas that match Silk Stilettos' actual content directly: **Fashion Designing**,
**Garment Construction & Manufacturing**, **Pattern Making**, and **Clothing Production &
Technology** all appear as standalone occupational areas in NCTVET's published Facilities
Standards list. This flips the earlier assumption (OCN as lead, CVQ as secondary) for this one
programme specifically.

| Brand Development & "Ebony Style" Craft | No direct CVQ match | Stays internal — brand identity work isn't a named occupational competency anywhere checked so far |
| Retail & Wall-Rental Practice (the Steps mechanism) | **Garment Construction & Manufacturing** (partial) | Loose fit at best — this module is closer to WW's own TECHreneurs-adjacent retail mechanics than any single CVQ area |
| Collection/Garment Production (capstone, portfolio via Joystick) | **Garment Construction & Manufacturing** | Direct named match for the production side of the capstone |

## What stays internal regardless of CVQ enrolment

Wearable Ergonomics, Costume Design for Performance, and Brand Development have no CVQ
counterpart and should remain WW-assessed modules. This mirrors the STEMgeneers/Roots pattern:
roughly half this syllabus has a real external home, and half is WW's own distinctive
contribution that no outside body names as a discrete competency.

## Recommended next step

Since CVQ operates through in-country Accredited Training Organisations (ATOs) rather than a
single UK-facing centre-approval process like OCN London, the practical next step is identifying
a real ATO (via NCTVET Jamaica or Trinidad's NTA) willing to partner on delivery or Prior
Learning Assessment and Recognition (PLAR) for members who already have relevant skill but no
formal credential — PLAR may be the faster route for members with existing sewing/pattern-making
experience rather than requiring a full training programme from scratch.
