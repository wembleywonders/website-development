This is a single bespoke unit, submitted to close a specific, confirmed gap in an otherwise
well-covered programme. STEMgeneers' remaining nine syllabus modules are already met by OCN

## Unit — Engineering Ethics & Professional Practice

**Level:** 2 | **Credit value:** 2 | **Guided Learning Hours:** 20

**Unit aim:** To introduce learners to the ethical and professional-conduct responsibilities of
engineering practice, connecting directly to WW's own craftsmanship-taken-seriously principle at
its Leader/deity tier.

**Learning outcome 1:** The learner will understand core principles of engineering ethics.

- 1.1 The learner can explain why engineers carry a professional responsibility to public
  safety, distinct from a general duty of care.
- 1.2 The learner can identify at least one real historical case where a failure of engineering
  ethics or professional judgement led to harm (e.g. a structural failure, a safety-critical
  software failure).
- 1.3 The learner can describe the concept of a professional code of conduct, with reference to
  a real UK engineering professional body (e.g. the Institution of Engineering and Technology,
  the Institution of Mechanical Engineers).

**Learning outcome 2:** The learner will understand ethical responsibility in a design or build
process.

- 2.1 The learner can identify at least two points in a design/build process where an ethical
  or safety judgement call must be made (e.g. material substitution under cost pressure,
  skipping a testing step under time pressure).
- 2.2 The learner can explain the concept of "duty to raise a concern" even where doing so is
  professionally uncomfortable or costly.
- 2.3 The learner can describe how transparency about a design limitation differs from
  concealment, using a worked example.

**Learning outcome 3:** The learner will be able to apply ethical reasoning to a real or
realistic engineering scenario.

- 3.1 The learner can analyse a short case study (real or realistic) involving a
  professional-practice or safety dilemma and identify the ethical issue at stake.
- 3.2 The learner can propose and justify a course of action for the case study, weighing
  safety, honesty, and professional responsibility.
- 3.3 The learner can reflect on how this connects to their own practice in a real WW build (e.g.
  a Scrap Cat project, a Hardware Lab prototype), identifying one point where they had to make —
  or would need to make — a similar judgement call.

**Assessment method:** A written case-study analysis (500–800 words) applying ethical reasoning
to a named scenario, plus a short reflective note connecting the analysis to the learner's own
STEMgeneers/Scrap Cat practical work.

## Note for OCN London's curriculum team

This unit is deliberately kept general rather than narrowly technical, since STEMgeneers members
work across multiple engineering disciplines (mechanical, electrical, materials) rather than
specialising in one. We anticipate this being one of the more portable bespoke units in our
submission set, potentially of interest to other centres delivering engineering qualifications to
non-traditional adult learners where a dedicated ethics unit is similarly absent from the
regulated suite.

## Internal verification notes (NOT part of the OCN submission — for WW's own record only)

- `accreditation/programmes/stemgeneers/` genuinely has existing `unit-mapping.md`,
  `assessment-criteria.md`, and `evidence-requirements.md` — this bespoke unit is additive to
  that content, not a replacement.
- **"Craftsmanship-taken-seriously principle" is real, but "Leader/deity tier" (as filed in the
  Unit aim above, kept exactly as drafted) blends two distinct WW concepts.**
  `docs/research/ww-cast-roster.md` confirms plagiarism and poor craftsmanship are treated
  seriously by an in-world "deity tier" (the Forge-Weaver and Long Memory personas, tied to the
  Creator's Code and Skunkworks/Impact Labs peer-review standards) — a genuine, if
  narrative-framed, WW concept. "Leader tier," by contrast, is the member-facing badge-progression
  tier (Explorer→Builder→Innovator→Leader). These are two separate layers the phrase runs
  together; worth knowing before this line reaches an external reviewer who may ask what "deity
  tier" refers to, since narrative-layer framing in a formal qualification submission may draw a
  question. Not corrected here per the established practice of filing external-facing content as
  drafted.
- **"Maker" as STEMgeneers' custodian** is consistent both with `src/utils/rovMapping.ts` (The
  Maker's `programmes` array includes `stemgeneers`, `scrap-cat`, `bright-sparks`) and the
  canonical 12-Children roster (Kofi owns STEMgeneers, "+ Scrap Cat, same domain") — no
  discrepancy here, unlike the "Keeper" ambiguity flagged in the Pageturners submission.
