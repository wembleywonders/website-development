## The £1,000 Question

### The Trading Allowance

You get a **£1,000 tax-free trading allowance** each tax year (6 April to 5 April).

This means:

- If your **total self-employment income** is under £1,000/year → No need to declare
- If it's **over £1,000/year** → You must register for Self Assessment

**Critical:** This is your TOTAL from ALL sources, not just Wembley Wonders.

### Example

| Marcus | £900 (Radio Host) | £200 (tutoring) | £1,100 | ✅ Yes |
| Precious | £1,200 (Archivist) | £0 | £1,200 | ✅ Yes |

- ✅ Selling on eBay, Etsy, Vinted (if over £1,000)

- ❌ Refunds or reimbursements

## Registering for Self Assessment

If your self-employment income exceeds £1,000, you need to:

### Step 1: Register with HMRC

- Go to: gov.uk/register-for-self-assessment
- You'll get a Unique Taxpayer Reference (UTR)
- This can take 2-4 weeks

### Step 2: File a Tax Return

- Deadline: 31 January (for the previous tax year)
- Online filing is easiest
- You report ALL your income (employed + self-employed)

### Step 3: Pay Any Tax Owed

- Also due 31 January
- You only pay tax on PROFIT, not total income (more on this below)

## The Magic of Expenses (Tax Efficiency)

**This is where most people leave money on the table.**

You don't pay tax on your total income. You pay tax on your **profit** (income minus allowable expenses).

### What Can You Claim?

As a Community Archivist or Creator, legitimate expenses might include:

| Expense | Example | Claimable? |
|---------|---------|------------|
| Travel to interviews | Bus fare, petrol, parking | ✅ Yes |
| Phone costs | Portion used for work | ✅ Yes (proportional) |
| Printing | Consent forms, guides | ✅ Yes |
| Stationery | Notebooks, pens | ✅ Yes |
| Equipment | Recorder, microphone | ✅ Yes |
| Software | Editing tools, apps | ✅ Yes |
| Home office | Portion of bills | ✅ Yes (simplified or actual) |
| Training | Courses for your work | ✅ Yes |
| Professional fees | Accountant | ✅ Yes |

### Example: Community Archivist

```
Income received from Wembley Wonders:    £1,440
                                         ------
Less allowable expenses:
  Travel (bus fares, petrol)              £180
  Phone (20% of £40/month bill)            £96
  Printing consent forms                   £30
  Notebook and pens                        £15
                                         ------
Total expenses:                           £321
                                         ------
TAXABLE PROFIT:                         £1,119
```

Instead of paying tax on £1,440, you pay tax on £1,119. That's a saving.

### Keep Receipts!

If HMRC asks, you need to prove your expenses. Keep:

- Receipts (photo them - paper fades)
- Bank statements
- Mileage log (if claiming travel)

**No receipt = no claim.**

## Simplified Expenses

HMRC offers simplified rates for some expenses:

### Vehicle Costs (if you drive to interviews)

- First 10,000 miles: 45p per mile
- Over 10,000 miles: 25p per mile

Just keep a log of business journeys.

### Working from Home

- 25-50 hours/month at home: £10/month
- 51-100 hours/month: £18/month
- 101+ hours/month: £26/month

No receipts needed for these - just use the flat rate.

## Making Tax Digital (MTD) - April 2026

From April 2026, if your self-employment income exceeds £50,000, you must:

- Use MTD-compatible software
- Submit quarterly updates (not just annual)
- Keep digital records

**Penalties for non-compliance are harsh.** This threshold will likely drop in future years, eventually catching more people.

If you're earning significantly through Wembley Wonders and other sources, start preparing now.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### 1. Ignoring the £1,000 Threshold

"I only made a bit" isn't a defence. Track your income properly.

### 2. Not Claiming Expenses

Most people don't claim what they're entitled to. You're giving HMRC free money.

### 3. Panicking at HMRC Letters

A letter doesn't mean you've done anything wrong. Read it carefully. Get advice before responding.

### 4. Mixing Personal and Business

Keep business income/expenses separate if possible. Makes everything easier.

### 5. Missing Deadlines

31 January is the deadline. Miss it and you get automatic penalties, even if you owe nothing.

## If You Get a Letter from HMRC

**Don't panic.** Many letters are "fishing" - they're asking questions, not accusing you.

1. **Read it carefully** - What are they actually asking?
2. **Don't rush to respond** - You usually have 30 days
3. **Don't volunteer extra information** - Answer only what's asked
4. **Get advice if unsure** - TaxAid (free for low income), accountant, or Citizens Advice
5. **Keep copies** - Of the letter and your response

If you've made a genuine mistake, honesty is usually the best policy - penalties are lower for voluntary disclosure than for being caught.
