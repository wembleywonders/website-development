# Pageturners — Composition Craft with Balla Fasséké
## Leader Tier, Lesson 1: Passing the Method to Someone Else's Subject

*Tutor: Balla Fasséké — 13th-century founder of the Kouyaté griot line, tutor to Sundiata Keita. His actual role in Mande tradition was teaching, not just performing: composed and preserved history for a purpose, in a form built to survive being carried in memory alone.*

---

### Before you start

Every tier so far has asked you to apply this method to a subject and meter *you* chose. Fasséké's own role wasn't just composing — it was teaching the craft to the next Kouyaté generation, on subjects the tradition needed covered, not just the ones a griot personally found easy. Leader tier is the same shift: you no longer pick the terrain. You teach the method itself, on someone else's subject, in a meter neither of you chose in advance.

This is a harder test than anything Innovator tier asked. Innovator tier proved you could judge your own bank as a piece grew. Leader tier proves the method itself — question, formula, bank, retirement — actually transfers to a mind that isn't yours, on material neither of you controls.

---

### 1. Why teaching cold is a different skill than composing well

You can be a genuinely good composer and a poor teacher of composition — the two skills aren't the same, and conflating them is a real risk at this tier. Teaching the method means making its logic visible to someone who's never seen it: not showing them a finished slot bank as an example, but walking them through *why* a slot works before either of you has built one together.

### 2. Not choosing the subject or meter is the actual test

Anyone can apply a method well to material they'd have chosen anyway. The real test of whether you understand the method — not just this craft — is whether it still works when the subject is imposed rather than picked. If the method only works on subjects you'd naturally gravitate to, you've learned a personal style, not Fasséké's actual teachable craft.

### 3. What "finished" means at this tier

A Leader-tier submission isn't finished when your own piece is good. It's finished when the person you taught can explain, in their own words, why their formula-slots fit their chosen meter — meaning they've internalised the *reasoning*, not just copied your process. That's the actual measure of whether the teaching worked.

### Try This Yourself (Leader tier evidence)

1. **Find someone to teach**, and let them (or a third party) pick the subject and meter — not you. If you can't find a real person, use the structured-pairing option other Template B lessons in this programme use: a scheduled session with a facilitator, where the facilitator or another participant sets the subject/meter.
2. **Teach the method live**, in order: the question the meter/formula serves, grounding the choice of meter in the subject, building a small bank, and at least one retirement decision if the piece runs long enough. Don't hand them a finished example first — walk them through building it.
3. **Have them explain it back**, in their own words, why their formula-slots fit their chosen meter. Write down what they said — not a paraphrase, their actual explanation.
4. **Write a short reflection**: where the teaching worked cleanly, and where you had to explain something twice or differently than you expected. A teaching session that went perfectly on the first try is less useful evidence than one where you had to actually adapt — note honestly if that happened.

Submit the teaching session record, the other person's own explanation in their words, and your reflection together as your Leader-tier evidence.

---

## Self-Check Before Submitting

- Did the other person choose the subject and meter, not you?
- Can you point to their own words explaining why their bank fits their meter — not your summary of what they probably understood?
- Did you note honestly where the teaching needed adapting, rather than presenting it as effortless?

## Submission Checklist

- [ ] Subject and meter chosen by someone other than you
- [ ] Teaching session conducted, method taught in order (question, formula, bank, retirement)
- [ ] Other person's own explanation recorded in their words
- [ ] Honest reflection on what worked and what needed adapting
