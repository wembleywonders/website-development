# Pageturners — Comedy & Characterisation Craft with Felix Dexter
## Leader Tier: Applying the Framework to Material That Isn't Yours

*Tutor: Felix Dexter (1961–2013) — solo character-act performer on* The Real
McCoy *and writer-performer on* Down the Line*; the seven principles applied
here are drawn from* Red Dwarf *and demonstrated directly in Dexter's own
broadcast work.*

## Unit 1 — Choosing Material You Didn't Grow Up With (≈20 min)

**Mini-objective:** be able to name a comedian or text genuinely new to you,
and state honestly what you don't yet know about it.

Every tier so far applied the seven principles to material you chose because
you already knew it, or to a piece you wrote yourself. Leader tier is the
same shift Balla Fasséké's own Leader tier makes: you no longer work only
with material you already understand. You pick — or are given — a comedian
or text you don't already know well, and find out whether the framework
still holds up when applied honestly to something new.

Choose a comedian, sitcom, or comedy text you haven't studied closely
before — not a childhood favourite, not something you've already half-
analysed in your head. State plainly what you don't yet know about it.

Comedian/text chosen: _________________________________________
What I don't yet know about their work: _______________________

**Mini-output — First Honest Impression:** Before deep analysis, write two
or three sentences on your first honest reaction to their work — including
if you don't find it funny, or find parts of it uncomfortable. An honest
first reaction is more useful evidence than a diplomatic one.

_____________________________________________________________

**Consolidation moment (≈2 min):** Without looking back, explain why
choosing unfamiliar material is the actual test at this tier, rather than
just "more analysis of the same kind."

My explanation, from memory: ________________________________

## Unit 2 — Applying All Seven, Honestly (≈20 min)

**Mini-objective:** be able to identify, for each of the seven principles,
whether your chosen comedian/text uses it, doesn't, or does something the
framework doesn't quite capture.

This is the real test of whether you understand the framework rather than
just Felix Dexter's own version of it: does it still work as an analytical
tool on someone else's comedy, including comedy built differently from
*Red Dwarf* or Dexter's own material? Some comedians will use all seven.
Some will use none of one or two, and use something the framework doesn't
name at all — that's a genuinely useful finding, not a failure of the
framework or your analysis.

Go through all seven, one at a time, and judge each honestly:

1. Anti-hero ensemble — present? YES / NO / PARTLY. Evidence: __________
2. Stakes without solemnity — present? YES / NO / PARTLY. Evidence: ____
3. Stasis as character engine — present? YES / NO / PARTLY. Evidence: __
4. Genre refusal — present? YES / NO / PARTLY. Evidence: _______________
5. Institutions mocked, not balanced — present? YES / NO / PARTLY.
   Evidence: ____________________________________________________
6. Comedy over unresolved isolation — present? YES / NO / PARTLY.
   Evidence: ____________________________________________________
7. Masculinity examined, not modelled — present? YES / NO / PARTLY.
   Evidence: ____________________________________________________

**Mini-output — Name What's Missing:** If any principle came back NO, or if
you noticed something this comedian/text does that none of the seven
principles quite captures, write it down. A real gap in the framework is
worth more at this tier than a forced YES on all seven.

_____________________________________________________________

**Consolidation moment (≈2 min):** Without looking back at your evidence,
state from memory which principle was the strongest fit and which was the
weakest, and why.

Strongest fit, from memory: ___________________________________
Weakest fit, from memory: _____________________________________

## Unit 3 — Writing the Analysis Up (≈20 min)

**Mini-objective:** be able to produce a written analysis clear enough that
someone who's never seen your chosen comedian's work could follow your
reasoning.

Explaining a framework well to someone unfamiliar with both the framework
and the material is a different skill from applying the framework
privately. Fasséké's own Leader tier makes the same point: understanding a
method and being able to make it visible to someone else are not the same
skill, and this tier tests the second one specifically.

**Mini-output — Draft the Analysis:** Write a short analysis (400+ words)
covering: who the comedian/text is and why you chose them; which of the
seven principles are present, with real evidence for each; anything the
framework didn't capture; and one honest paragraph on what applying this
framework to unfamiliar material taught you that applying it to *Red Dwarf*
or your own work didn't. Quote no more than 15 words from any single source
in your analysis, per this programme's sourcing rule.

[Write your analysis on a separate page or document.]

**Consolidation moment (≈2 min):** Without re-reading it, state in one
sentence what your analysis's central claim is — the one thing a reader
should take away.

My central claim, from memory: ________________________________

## Self-Assessment Checklist (Leader tier evidence)

- [ ] Comedian/text chosen was genuinely unfamiliar, not previously studied
      or a childhood favourite
- [ ] Honest first impression recorded before deep analysis (Unit 1)
- [ ] All seven principles judged individually with real evidence,
      including any genuine NO or PARTLY answers (Unit 2)
- [ ] Any gap the framework didn't capture named honestly, not forced into
      one of the seven
- [ ] Written analysis (400+ words) completed, readable by someone
      unfamiliar with both the framework and the material, quoting no more
      than 15 words from any single source

If all five are checked, this document plus your written analysis **is**
your Leader-tier submission. Whether it becomes credited module content
alongside this lesson is a separate curator decision made after review —
completing the checklist is the evidence requirement, not a guarantee of
publication.
