# Pageturners — Comedy & Characterisation Craft with Felix Dexter
## Builder Tier: Writing With At Least Two Principles on Purpose

*Tutor: Felix Dexter (1961–2013) — solo character-act performer on* The Real
McCoy *and writer-performer on* Down the Line*; the seven principles applied
here are drawn from* Red Dwarf *and demonstrated directly in Dexter's own
broadcast work.*

## Unit 1 — Choosing Your Two Principles Deliberately (≈20 min)

**Mini-objective:** be able to name two principles you're choosing on
purpose, and explain in one sentence each why they suit the piece you're
about to write.

Explorer tier asked you to *spot* the seven principles in scenes you already
knew or invented. Builder tier asks something harder: pick two on purpose,
before you write a word of the piece, and let that choice shape what you
write — not the other way round.

Not every pairing works equally well together. Some worked examples of
principles that reinforce each other:

- **Anti-hero ensemble + stasis as character engine** — a group of people who
  never grow, colliding with each other every episode. This is *Red Dwarf*'s
  actual combination.
- **Institutions mocked, not balanced + genre refusal** — a satire that can
  suddenly turn bleak mid-scene without ever softening its target. This is
  close to *Down the Line*'s combination.
- **Masculinity examined, not modelled + comedy over unresolved isolation** —
  a character whose insecurity or vanity is played entirely for laughs, with
  the loneliness underneath it never resolved or fixed by the story.

**Mini-output — Declare Your Pair:** Name your subject for this piece in one
sentence (a workplace, a household, a group of friends — anything). Then
name your two chosen principles and, for each, one sentence on why it suits
this specific subject.

Subject: ______________________________________________________
Principle 1 chosen: ___________________________________________
Why it suits this subject: ____________________________________
Principle 2 chosen: ___________________________________________
Why it suits this subject: ____________________________________

**Consolidation moment (≈2 min):** Without looking back, explain why
choosing your principles *before* writing produces a different piece than
writing first and labelling the principles afterward.

My explanation, from memory: ________________________________

## Unit 2 — Building the Piece Around the Pair (≈20 min)

**Mini-objective:** be able to draft an original scene where both chosen
principles are doing real, visible work — not just present in the
background.

A principle only counts as "applied" if removing it would make the scene
worse in a specific, nameable way. If a scene would work identically without
one of your two chosen principles, it isn't actually load-bearing yet — it's
decoration.

Test before you draft: for each of your two principles, write one sentence
stating what would break in your scene if you removed it. If you can't
answer that, the principle isn't structural yet — go back and rebuild the
scene around it properly.

If I removed Principle 1, what would break: __________________
If I removed Principle 2, what would break: __________________

**Mini-output — Draft the Piece:** Write an original character-comedy piece
(a sketch, a short ensemble scene, or a character study — your choice of
form) of at least 300 words, visibly built around your two chosen
principles. Original characters and situations only — do not reuse *Red
Dwarf* characters, scenarios, or dialogue, and do not quote Felix Dexter's
own material; this is your own piece using the craft, not a tribute to the
source texts.

[Write your piece on a separate page or document — this workbook holds the
planning and self-check only.]

**Consolidation moment (≈2 min):** Without re-reading your own piece, state
from memory the single moment in it where each of your two principles is
doing the most visible work.

Principle 1's clearest moment, from memory: __________________
Principle 2's clearest moment, from memory: __________________

## Unit 3 — Testing What You Built (≈20 min)

**Mini-objective:** be able to honestly judge whether your piece actually
uses its two principles structurally, and revise one weak spot.

Read your full piece back, out loud if possible. For each of your two
principles, find the exact line or moment where it's doing its work, and
check it against the test from Unit 2 — would removing it genuinely break
something, or could you delete it and the scene would read the same?

Principle 1 — exact line/moment: _____________________________
Does removing it break something specific? YES / NO — what, precisely?
_____________________________________________________________

Principle 2 — exact line/moment: _____________________________
Does removing it break something specific? YES / NO — what, precisely?
_____________________________________________________________

If either answer is NO, revise that section now before submitting — a
principle that isn't structurally load-bearing yet is worth fixing at
Builder tier, before the habit sets in.

**Mini-output — Revision Note:** Write two sentences: what you changed (if
anything) after this test, and why.

_____________________________________________________________
_____________________________________________________________

**Consolidation moment (≈2 min):** Without looking back at your revision
note, restate in one sentence what "structurally load-bearing" means for a
comic principle, as opposed to just being present.

My explanation, from memory: ________________________________

## Self-Assessment Checklist (Builder tier evidence)

- [ ] Two principles named and justified against my chosen subject, before
      drafting (Unit 1)
- [ ] Original piece drafted (300+ words), built around both principles,
      using no reused characters/scenarios/dialogue from *Red Dwarf* or
      Dexter's own material
- [ ] Both principles tested against the "would removing it break something
      specific" check (Unit 3), with at least one honest revision made if
      either failed the test
- [ ] Written in my own words and my own piece, not adapted from source
      material

If all four are checked, this document plus your original piece **is** your
Builder-tier submission.
