# Pageturners — Comedy & Characterisation Craft with Felix Dexter
## Innovator Tier: Making the Craft Travel

*Tutor: Felix Dexter (1961–2013) — solo character-act performer on* The Real
McCoy *and writer-performer on* Down the Line*; the seven principles applied
here are drawn from* Red Dwarf *and demonstrated directly in Dexter's own
broadcast work.*

---

**[REAL TOOL]:** Unchanged, re-checked before writing this lesson: no
comedy-craft analysis mechanism exists anywhere on the platform, and no
automated cross-programme content pipeline exists either — "feeding a piece
into another programme's content" here means writing a genuine adaptation

## Unit 1 — What "Travelling" Actually Requires (≈20 min)

**Mini-objective:** be able to name, precisely, what changes and what must
stay fixed when a piece moves from one format to another.

Builder tier proved your two chosen principles were structurally load-
bearing in one piece, in one format. Innovator tier asks a different
question: do the same principles still do their work when the surrounding
format changes underneath them? A character study built for a written
sketch doesn't automatically work as a spoken-word bit for Trubble n Bass,
or a scene beat for Easy Street's ongoing story — the *principles* can
travel, but the *piece* usually can't, unadapted.

Choose your destination format now, from the confirmed-live options (Easy
Street, a scene/character beat; Trubble n Bass, a spoken or musical bit) or
Joystick with the honesty caveat above.

Destination chosen: ___________________________________________
What does this format require that my original piece's format didn't (e.g.
spoken rhythm, a fit with an existing character's voice, a shorter runtime)?
_____________________________________________________________

**Mini-output — Name What Must Survive:** Of your two Builder-tier
principles, write one sentence each on what would have to survive the
adaptation for the piece to still count as using that principle at all.

Principle 1 — what must survive the format change: ____________
Principle 2 — what must survive the format change: ____________

**Consolidation moment (≈2 min):** Without looking back, explain in your own
words the difference between a principle travelling and a piece travelling.

My explanation, from memory: ________________________________

## Unit 2 — Adapting the Piece (≈20 min)

**Mini-objective:** be able to produce a genuine adaptation of your
Builder-tier piece to the new format, not a copy-paste with a new label.

An adaptation that changes nothing but the header isn't evidence of anything
at this tier. The real test: could someone who only knows the destination
programme's existing content recognise your adapted piece as belonging
there, on its own terms — not as a visitor from somewhere else?

**Mini-output — Draft the Adaptation:** Write the adapted piece, in the
destination format, keeping both chosen principles structurally load-bearing
(same "would removing it break something specific" test from Builder tier
still applies). Original material only, same rule as Builder tier.

[Write your adapted piece on a separate page or document.]

**Consolidation moment (≈2 min):** Without re-reading it, name the single
biggest change you made to fit the new format, and why it was necessary.

My biggest change, from memory: ______________________________

## Unit 3 — Judging the Fit Honestly (≈20 min)

**Mini-objective:** be able to give an honest verdict on whether the
adaptation actually works in its new home, and say what you'd change if it
doesn't yet.

Read the adapted piece back as if you were a member of the destination
programme who's never seen your original. Does it read as belonging there,
or does it still read like a piece from somewhere else wearing a different
label?

Honest verdict: BELONGS THERE / STILL READS AS BORROWED

If "still reads as borrowed," name the one specific thing that gives it
away (a word choice, a rhythm, a reference that doesn't fit) and what you'd
change:
_____________________________________________________________

**Mini-output — Submission Note:** Write two sentences: which destination
you chose and why, and one honest thing you learned about your own writing
by having to fit it somewhere else's rules.

_____________________________________________________________
_____________________________________________________________

**Consolidation moment (≈2 min):** Without looking back, state in one
sentence why "does it belong there" is a harder and more useful test than
"did I follow the format's rules."

My explanation, from memory: ________________________________

## Self-Assessment Checklist (Innovator tier evidence)

- [ ] Destination format chosen and named, with the Joystick caveat
      acknowledged if relevant
- [ ] Both Builder-tier principles' "what must survive" stated before
      adapting
- [ ] Adapted piece drafted in the new format, both principles still
      structurally load-bearing
- [ ] Honest fit verdict given, with a named specific reason if the piece
      still reads as borrowed
- [ ] Submission note completed, naming one real thing learned from the
      adaptation

If all five are checked, this document plus your adapted piece **is** your
Innovator-tier submission. Curator review — not any automated pipeline —
decides whether the adaptation is strong enough to actually be offered to
the destination programme's own content, and that offer is a separate,
later step, not part of this tier's evidence requirement.
