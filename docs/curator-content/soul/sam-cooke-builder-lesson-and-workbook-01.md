# Soul — Direct Coaching & Platform-Building with Sam Cooke
## Builder Tier, Lesson 1: Both Skills, Sustained

*Tutor: Sam Cooke — co-founded SAR Records in 1961 specifically to give Black artists a venue during the racially charged 1960s. Tirelessly coached vocalists during SAR sessions, emphasising diction; audible on surviving tape coaching artists between takes. Bobby Womack, who came up under him: "by him being there with you and just giving you that special attention, you wanted to give it to him like it was supposed to be. I think that's what made SAR, SAR."*

## TRACK ONE — COACHING

### 1. Sustained diction work, not a single note

Explorer tier asked for one specific, checkable diction note. Builder tier asks for what actually made SAR sessions work: Cooke coaching the same vocalist across a real recording process, not a single pass. The test is the same one Dodd's lesson uses, because it's the same underlying discipline — could you name, in one sentence per session, exactly what changed?

### 2. Why one good note doesn't make a coach

A single correct diction note proves you can hear the problem. A sustained coaching relationship proves you can track whether your notes actually land — whether the vocalist's diction genuinely improves take to take, or whether you're repeating the same note without adjusting your approach when it doesn't work.

### Try This Yourself — Coaching (Builder tier evidence, Track One)

1. **Solo, safe:** Pick a recording you know well with more than one released take or live version, if one exists (or two different performances of the same song by the same artist). Compare diction across them in writing — did it change take to take, performance to performance? What specifically differs?
2. **The real thing (organic path, default, or structured-pairing path — a scheduled, facilitated session, added 22 Aug 2026, if you don't have an obvious member to coach or want a genuine third-party witness):** with permission, coach the same member's vocal work across at least three real sessions. Log each: the specific diction note given, what changed by the next session, and what to focus on next. Reference each session against the one before it. Close with confirmation of what changed — the coached member's own written line (organic), or the facilitator's sign-off (structured-pairing).

## TRACK TWO — PLATFORM-BUILDING

### 1. From offering a slot to running one

Explorer tier asked you to describe a real or planned platform-offering act. Builder tier asks for the sustained version: not a single share or feature, but something closer to what SAR actually was — an ongoing space you maintain, where more than one piece of someone else's work has genuinely been heard because of it.

### 2. Why a platform is judged by what it does for others, not by existing

SAR wasn't evidence because Cooke owned a record label. It was evidence because real artists' real work reached real audiences through it, repeatedly. A Builder-tier platform-building act needs the same test: name, specifically, whose work reached whom, and how you know it actually reached them (a listen count, a direct response, a booking that followed) — not just that the offer was made.

### Try This Yourself — Platform-Building (Builder tier evidence, Track Two)

1. **Solo, safe:** Look at one real, small existing platform (a programme's showcase page, a community playlist, any feature space already on WW) and write two sentences on what makes it work as a platform — what does it actually do for the people featured on it, beyond just existing?
2. **The real thing:** Maintain or build a real, small ongoing space for someone else's work across at least two instances (two features, two shares, two slots offered and used) — not one-off. Log each instance: whose work, what you did, and how you know it reached someone (be specific and honest — "I think more people saw it" isn't evidence; a number, a message, or a direct response is).
