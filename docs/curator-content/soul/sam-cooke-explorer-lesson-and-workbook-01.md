## Unit 1 — Two Techniques Wearing One Name (≈20 min)

**Mini-objective:** be able to explain why coaching and platform-building are two distinct skills in Cooke's own history, and complete a solo diction-training exercise.

This one's genuinely two techniques wearing one name, and it's worth separating them before you try to practice either.

**Coaching** is direct, personal, relationship-based — you can't do it to an empty room. **Platform-building** is closer to institution-building — creating a real space (a label, a showcase, a channel) where other people's work can be heard. They're connected in Cooke's own life — he built SAR specifically *because* coaching alone wasn't enough; talented people still needed somewhere to actually be heard — but they're different muscles, and this lesson trains them separately before showing you why he needed both. As with the other relationship-based techniques in this programme: no clean, always-right upload mechanism exists yet for coaching evidence specifically — the written-account method from Coxsone Dodd's lesson applies here too.

**What Cooke was actually coaching for.** Cooke's own recorded sessions reveal what he was listening for, and it wasn't "sing better" in some vague sense. His focus, tape after tape, was **diction** — whether every word could actually be understood, whether the emotional intention of a line survived being sung rather than spoken. Try this test on any recorded vocal performance you know well: can you write down the lyrics accurately just from listening, without looking them up? If not — that's not necessarily bad singing, but it is exactly the kind of thing Cooke spent hours fixing on tape.

**Why platform-building isn't a separate hobby — it's what coaching is *for*.** SAR Records existed because Cooke understood something coaching alone couldn't fix: you can perfect someone's diction all day, but if no venue will book them and no label will sign them, the coaching doesn't reach an audience. Bobby Womack didn't just get vocal notes from Cooke — he got a label willing to put his group's record out.

**Why "I gave good notes" isn't evidence, and neither is "I made a page for someone."** Same discipline as the Dodd lesson, applied to two skills instead of one: a coaching note has to be specific enough that someone else could verify the change happened. A platform-building act has to be a real, functioning space — not a one-off shout-out, but something a second person could actually use.

## Between-Session Task — no fixed time box

This is the Explorer-tier requirement itself, for both halves of Cooke's technique. Neither fits a fixed window — the coaching note requires a real member's real work with permission; the platform description requires either a real act or an honest plan.

1. **The coaching note.** With permission, listen to one other member's early vocal work. Give ONE specific, checkable diction note — not general encouragement — and write it down, dated.
2. **The platform description.** Describe, honestly, any real space — however small — you could offer or have offered someone else's work (a share, a feature, a mention, a slot). If you haven't done this yet, write what you *would* offer and to whom, as a plan rather than a claim.

Come back to the return unit once at least the coaching note has actually happened.
