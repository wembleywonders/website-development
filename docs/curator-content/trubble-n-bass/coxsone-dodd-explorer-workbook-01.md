# Trubble n Bass — Activity Workbook
## Artist Development & A&R with Coxsone Dodd | Explorer Tier, Lesson 1

*Companion to: "The Skill Nobody Names." Complete after reading the lesson — this workbook assumes you already understand why this evidence type is written accounts, not uploaded files.*

---

### A note before you start

Every other workbook in this programme opens with "pick something you already know and practice on it alone." This one can't, honestly, and it's worth saying why rather than pretending otherwise: Dodd's actual skill only exists in contact with someone else's real, unfinished work. You can't practice recognising raw talent against a blank page the way you can practice marking a stress pattern.

So this workbook does something different. It builds the *listening habit* first, using material that already exists and needs no permission to use — then sends you toward the real thing.

Time estimate: 30–40 minutes for Part One (solo). Part Two requires two other people and can't be timed the same way — do it when it's genuinely possible, not to a deadline.

---

## Part One — Solo: Train the Ear (do this before Part Two)

You can't practice A&R alone. But you *can* practice the specific listening skill underneath it, on material where nobody's feelings are at stake.

### Activity 1 — Distinct vs. Good

Think of three pieces of music, writing, or any creative work you've encountered recently — doesn't matter where from, doesn't need to be "amateur." For each, answer both questions separately. This is the core distinction the lesson drew: distinct isn't the same as good.

Piece 1: ___________________________________________
Is it GOOD? (polished, well-executed) YES / NO
Is it DISTINCT? (doing something no one else's does) YES / NO

Piece 2: ___________________________________________
Is it GOOD? YES / NO
Is it DISTINCT? YES / NO

Piece 3: ___________________________________________
Is it GOOD? YES / NO
Is it DISTINCT? YES / NO

**Question:** Did any of your three score YES on distinct but NO on good — or the reverse? Circle: **YES / NO**

If yes, write one sentence about that piece specifically:

_____________________________________________________

That gap — distinct-but-rough, or polished-but-forgettable — is exactly what Dodd was listening past and for. If none of your three showed the gap, that's fine; try the activity again later with three different pieces until one does. The gap is what you're training yourself to notice.

### Activity 2 — Name the Specific Thing

Pick whichever piece from Activity 1 felt most distinct, whether or not it was also good.

Write ONE sentence naming the *specific* thing that makes it distinct — not "it's good" or "I like it," but the actual mechanism. (Worked example: not "the singer has a great voice," but "the singer holds notes half a beat longer than expected, which makes ordinary lines feel unresolved.")

Your sentence: ______________________________________
_____________________________________________________

**Self-check:** Read your sentence back. Does it name something *specific enough that someone else could recognise it too*, or is it just praise? Circle: **SPECIFIC / JUST PRAISE**

If "just praise," rewrite it once more, forcing yourself to name the mechanism:

_____________________________________________________

## Part Two — With Real People (the actual evidence)

This is not optional padding — it's the actual technique. Part One trained the ear; this is where the craft itself happens.

### Step 1 — Observe

With their permission, watch or listen to two members' early-stage work, at any tier, in this or any programme.

For each, using what you trained in Part One:

Member/piece A: ____________________________________
Distinct thing under the rough edges: ______________
What specifically would need to change: ____________

Member/piece B: ____________________________________
Distinct thing under the rough edges: ______________
What specifically would need to change: ____________

This is your Explorer-tier evidence — the written account itself, dated, exactly as the lesson described. Use this space, or attach a separate page if you need more room:

## Self-Assessment Checklist (Explorer tier evidence)

- [ ] Completed Activity 1 and 2 (Part One) honestly, including at least one attempt at naming a *specific* mechanism, not just praise
- [ ] Observed two real members' early-stage work with their permission (Part Two, Step 1)
- [ ] Written a dated account naming what you noticed and what you'd work on (Part Two, Step 2)

If all three are checked, Part Two's written account **is** your Explorer-tier submission — Part One is training, not evidence in itself, since it doesn't involve a real developing relationship.
