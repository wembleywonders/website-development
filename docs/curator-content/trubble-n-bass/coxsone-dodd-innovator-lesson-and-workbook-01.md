# Trubble n Bass — Artist Development & A&R with Coxsone Dodd
## Innovator Tier: The Formal Record, and Knowing When to Walk Away

*Curator: Coxsone Dodd — founded Studio One in 1963, personally auditioned and developed hundreds of artists including a young Bob Marley, mentored Freddie McGregor directly. His technique isn't songwriting or engineering — it's recognising raw ability before anyone else will, and then actually building around it.*

---

### Before you start — this tier maps to a real accreditation criterion

Innovator tier for this curator isn't a stylistic choice — it's TNB-2.4 (Artist Development & A&R), a real criterion in `accreditation/programmes/trubble-n-bass/evidence-requirements.md`, and it names its evidence types precisely: an **Artist development record** ("documented development process") and the **Developed member's confirmation** ("peer-witness statement from the member developed"). That's exactly what Builder tier's write-up already started training — this tier asks for the sustained, formally-structured version.

**A correction, checked directly before this lesson was written, not assumed:** that same accreditation document states Criterion 2.4 "is verified via the platform's existing peer-witness requirement (required for Practitioner level and above platform-wide)." This claim was checked in an earlier session and found inaccurate — no unified, platform-wide peer-witness mechanism actually exists. What's really there is three separate, disconnected verification models (Scrap-Cat-rooted `witnessRepair()` in `journalStore.ts`; a `PrototypeLab.tsx` reuse of that same Scrap Cat mechanism for STEMgeneers; and a third, differently-worded system in `BadgeProgress.tsx` not clearly wired to either). The same document also cites "the Crew Log's 'peer-facing showcase' touchpoint... see ww-skunkworks-impact-labs.md" — that file does not exist anywhere in this repo, and no "Skunkworks"/"Crew Log" cross-skill rotation gate exists in code at all. This lesson does not repeat either claim as true. The evidence path below is the same honest written-record method Explorer and Builder tier used, now structured to match TNB-2.4's two named evidence types directly — not because a platform mechanism verifies it, but because that's genuinely what the criterion asks for, mechanism or not.

### 1. What "the formal record" actually adds beyond Builder tier's log

Builder tier asked for a three-session log and one confirmation line. TNB-2.4's own evidence types ask for something slightly more structured: a **development record** that reads as a real account of a process (not just session notes — a document someone else, like an assessor, could pick up and understand what happened and why), and a **confirmation** that's genuinely the developed member's own account, not a form they signed. The difference is presentation and completeness, not a new skill — but presentation is part of the craft. Dodd didn't just do the development work; Studio One's whole existence depended on the results being legible to other people (audiences, radio, other producers), not just true in his own head.

### 2. Knowing when to walk away — the judgment Builder tier didn't ask for

Real A&R history isn't only success stories. Part of the actual skill is recognising when continued investment isn't the right call — not because the person lacks worth, but because this specific development relationship, at this specific time, has stopped producing real change. This is a harder discrimination than Builder tier's "spot the gap" pattern recognition: it requires judging your own ongoing work honestly, not just analysing someone else's history from a safe distance.

A useful test, extending Builder tier's "name the exact change" standard: if you can't name a specific change across your last two sessions, that's not automatically a failure — it might be a real plateau (which Builder tier already taught you to log honestly rather than paper over). But if you *also* can't name a specific next thing worth trying, that combination — no recent change, no clear next move — is the actual signal that this relationship may have reached its honest limit for now. Recognising that and saying so plainly is not a lesser outcome than a success story. It's the same discipline in reverse.

### 3. Why walking away well is part of the craft, not a failure to hide

A coach who never says "I don't think I'm the right person to keep working with you on this" either never develops anyone difficult enough to test that judgment, or is dishonest about outcomes that didn't work. Neither is Dodd's real legacy — his own history includes people he passed on, not just people he found. Recording an honest "this didn't continue, and here's why" is real evidence of the craft, not an admission of its absence.

### Try This Yourself (Innovator tier evidence)

1. **Continue or start a real development relationship** — the same person from Builder tier if the relationship is still active and productive, or a new one if that relationship reached its honest limit (see Part Two above; if so, write that ending up first, honestly, as its own short record).
2. **Write the formal development record.** Structured, dated, readable by someone who wasn't there: what you were working on, the real sessions and what happened in each, and where things stand now. This is TNB-2.4's "Artist development record" evidence type, written for real.
3. **Get the developed member's confirmation**, in their own words, as TNB-2.4's second evidence type — organic (their own written line) or structured-pairing (facilitator sign-off), same two paths as Builder tier.
4. **If the relationship reached its honest limit rather than a success**, that's a valid, complete submission on its own — write plainly what you tried, what changed and what didn't, and why you believe continuing wouldn't currently help. This is not a lesser version of the evidence.

Submit the development record and the confirmation (or the honest ending, if that's what happened) together as your Innovator-tier evidence.

## Self-Check Before Submitting

- Does your development record read like something someone who wasn't there could actually follow?
- Did you get real confirmation — the developed member's own words, or a facilitator's sign-off — not a summary you wrote for them?
- If the relationship ended without a clean success, did you write that honestly rather than stretching the evidence to look more finished than it was?
- Did you avoid citing "the platform's peer-witness requirement" or "Skunkworks/Crew Log" as if either backs this up — neither does, as checked above?
