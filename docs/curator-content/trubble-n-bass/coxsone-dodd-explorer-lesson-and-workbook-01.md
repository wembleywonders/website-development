## Unit 1 — Train the Ear (≈20 min)

**Mini-objective:** be able to name the specific, checkable difference between "distinct" and "good," and explain why Dodd's evidence path is a written account rather than an uploaded file.

Every other tutor in this programme teaches you to make something: a beat, a mix, a melody. You'll walk away from their lessons with a file you can point to.

Dodd's craft doesn't work that way. What he built wasn't a sound — it was *other people's sounds*, ones that didn't exist yet when he found them. Bob Marley walked into a Sunday audition as a raw teenager with something unformed. Dodd's actual skill was recognizing what that unformed thing could become, and then doing the specific, patient work to help it get there. That's a real, teachable craft — but it produces no audio file of its own. It produces *someone else's* better work.

**This matters for how you'll be assessed, and it's worth saying plainly: the platform doesn't yet have a proper way to log this kind of evidence.** Every other technique in this programme has a clean digital trail — a file gets uploaded, timestamped, done. A&R doesn't leave that kind of trail on its own. What you'll be asked to do in the between-session task below is closer to what actually verified Dodd's own skill historically: not a recording, but *someone else's account of what happened*, in their own words. Until a better mechanism exists, that account — properly written down, dated, and ideally confirmed by the person you worked with — is the record.

**What Dodd was actually listening for.** Dodd's Sunday auditions weren't polished showcases — most of what walked through his door was rough. His actual skill wasn't spotting *finished* talent; almost anyone can hear that. His skill was hearing past the rough edges to something specific and real underneath them. Three things he was documented listening for, distilled into something you can practice: a voice or idea doing something nobody else's does, even badly — not "good," *distinct*; a gap between what someone's attempting and what they're actually capable of; and whether the person could be worked with, since talent that can't take direction is a dead end for this specific craft.

**The actual mechanism: audition, then invest.** Stage one — the audition — is low-stakes, high-volume, deliberately open to almost anyone; the point isn't finding someone ready, it's finding someone *worth the time it would take to make them ready*. Stage two — the development — is where the real craft lives: with Freddie McGregor specifically, this wasn't occasional advice, it was sustained, personal, over real time, in Dodd's own home.

**Why "I told them they were good" isn't evidence.** A useful test: could you write down, in one sentence, *exactly what changed* in the other person's work because of what you did? "I told them I liked it" fails that test. "I noticed their timing rushed on the second verse, showed them how to count it out loud, and they fixed it on the next take" passes it.

**Mini-output — Distinct vs. Good:** Think of three pieces of music, writing, or any creative work you've encountered recently. For each, answer both questions separately.

Piece 1: ________ | GOOD? YES/NO | DISTINCT? YES/NO
Piece 2: ________ | GOOD? YES/NO | DISTINCT? YES/NO
Piece 3: ________ | GOOD? YES/NO | DISTINCT? YES/NO

Did any score YES on distinct but NO on good, or the reverse? _____________

Pick whichever felt most distinct. Write ONE sentence naming the *specific* mechanism that makes it distinct — not "it's good," the actual thing it does:

_____________________________________________________

**Consolidation moment (≈2 min):** Without looking back, state the difference between "distinct" and "good" in your own words, and why Dodd's evidence path is a written account rather than a file upload.

## Between-Session Task — no fixed time box

This is the Explorer-tier requirement itself. It requires real access to two other members' real, raw work, with their permission — not something to force into a fixed window.

1. **Observe, don't yet act.** Watch or listen to two members' raw, early-stage work (with their permission), anything from this programme, at any tier.
2. For each, using what you trained in Unit 1: name the *distinct* thing under the rough edges, and what specifically would need to change for it to land.
3. **Write the account.** Write it exactly as if you were Dodd describing a Sunday audition to someone else: who you heard, what you actually noticed, and what you'd want to work on with them if given the chance. Date it.

Come back to the return unit once this has actually happened.
