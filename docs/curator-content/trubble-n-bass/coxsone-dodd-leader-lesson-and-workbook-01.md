Dodd's Leader-tier evidence is that connection made real — the artist he coached under TNB-2.4 actually reaching TNB-3's real steps, as a downstream outcome of his coaching, credited honestly as an outcome of A&R work, not as Dodd teaching production or distribution craft himself.

### 1. What "reaching a real audience" adds beyond Innovator tier

Innovator tier's evidence was a formal development record and a confirmation — real, but still private, between Dodd and the person he developed. Leader tier asks whether that private development work produced something that could survive contact with an actual audience: airplay, a published portfolio entry, real listens. Studio One's whole reputation depended on this — a Sunday audition that never led anywhere public wasn't the story people remember; Bob Marley reaching a real audience is.

### 2. Your role at this stage, honestly stated

You are not the one submitting the track, mastering it to spec, or publishing the Joystick entry — that's the developed artist's own craft-based work, assessed under TNB-3's real criteria by whoever owns that content. Your role is what got them ready to attempt it, and your evidence at this tier is honest testimony to that readiness, not a claim on their production credit.

### 3. Why an honest "not there yet" is still real Leader-tier evidence

Not every development relationship reaches a public outcome on any particular timeline, and forcing a premature submission just to complete this tier would be dishonest in exactly the way Innovator tier already warned against. If the person you developed genuinely isn't ready for TNB-3's real steps yet, the honest account of that — what's ready, what isn't, and why — is real evidence of mature A&R judgment, not a failure to produce a result on schedule.

### Try This Yourself (Leader tier evidence)

1. **Return to the person you developed** (from Builder/Innovator tier, or a continuation).
2. **If they've reached readiness**: support them through submitting to TNB-3's real steps — Rayd-yo airplay submission and/or a Joystick portfolio entry — as their own work, under their own name. Write a short account connecting your development record to their outcome: what you did, and what they went on to achieve.
3. **If they haven't reached readiness yet**: write an honest account of where things actually stand — what's ready, what specifically isn't, and what you believe still needs to happen. This is a complete, valid Leader-tier submission on its own.
4. **Either way, get their own account** of what your coaching specifically contributed to wherever they've reached — in their own words, not yours.

Submit the connecting account (readiness reached, or honest not-yet) and the developed member's own account of your contribution together as your Leader-tier evidence.
