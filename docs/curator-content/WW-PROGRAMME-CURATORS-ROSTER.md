**Added here (21 Aug 2026, co-tutors alongside Fasséké, not replacing him):**

### Muhammad Baghayogho (d. 1593) + Ahmad Baba al-Tinbukti (1556–1627)

Opens the Islamic/West African written scholarly tradition thread — a real counterpart to
Fasséké's oral griot line: two transmission modes (oral and text-based), giving
Pageturners both honestly rather than only one.

- **Baghayogho** — direct teaching credential. Timbuktu scholar based at Sankore,
  renowned for legal and linguistic texts, honorary doctorate conferred by Al-Azhar
  University in Cairo. Real, documented mentorship: his student Ahmad Baba al-Tinbukti
  became Timbuktu's most celebrated scholar and explicitly called Baghayogho "the greatest
  scholar and renewer of the 16th century."
- **Ahmad Baba** — prolific output plus real counter-memory resonance with WW's own
  mission. Authored 40+ works; explicitly and publicly defended the intellectual capacity
  of Black Africans against contemporary racist claims; imprisoned by invading Moroccan
  forces after the 1591 conquest of Timbuktu (political persecution, not misconduct).
- Both real historical figures at Sankore, one of the oldest centres of higher learning in
  the world (peak enrolment ~25,000 students, 300,000–700,000 surviving manuscripts).
- **Conduct caveats:** none on either.
- **Provenance note:** these two were originally researched for STEMgeneers, then
  correctly reassigned here after it became clear their real output is legal/theological
  scholarship, not STEM content — see the outstanding-tasks tracker for that correction's
  history (not independently re-traced for this file).

**Added here (21 Aug 2026, co-tutors alongside Demodocus, not replacing him):**

### Paul Nabor (1928–2014) + Andy Palacio (1960–2008)

Opens the Garifuna thread — an Afro-Indigenous people (African and Carib/Arawak ancestry,
exiled from Saint Vincent in 1797, now spread across Belize, Honduras, Guatemala,
Nicaragua) essentially invisible in standard "Caribbean" framing, whose oral-performance
tradition maps directly onto Demodocus's own role.

- **Nabor** — elder oral/musical tradition-keeper. Master of paranda (the acoustic,
  solo-guitar, call-and-response precursor to Punta), composing and performing from the
  1940s onward. **Honest caveat:** teaching credential is influence-through-presence over
  decades rather than formally documented named mentorship — real and multi-generational,
  but thinner than a named-successor claim.
- **Palacio** — institution-building/global platform. Started as a high school social
  studies teacher; hosted a Garifuna-language radio programme from 1981 specifically to
  keep the culture and language alive for the next generation; became Belize's official
  Cultural Ambassador and head of the National Institute of Culture and History.
  Deliberately built the Garifuna Collective as an intergenerational band, bringing elder
  Nabor together with rising young musician Aurelio Martinez rather than performing solo —
  a structural act of succession-building. His 2007 album *Wátina* is widely credited as
  the work that carried Garifuna language and culture to a global audience for the first
  time; named a UNESCO Artist for Peace the same year.
- **Conduct caveats:** none. Palacio died suddenly of a stroke in 2008, natural causes.

**Added here (21 Aug 2026, co-tutor alongside Nat D. Williams, not replacing him):**

### Eusebia Cosme (1908–1976)

Cross-lingual broadcasting bridge, opening the Afro-Cuban/wider Hispanic-Caribbean thread
on this programme.

Born to a Black working-class family in Santiago de Cuba; Cuban theatre's racial
segregation shut her out of traditional acting roles, so she built her own path through
declamación (dramatic poetry recitation), becoming the leading interpreter of
Afro-Antillean verse across Latin America, the Caribbean, Europe, and the US. From 1943–45
hosted her own show on CBS Radio's *Las Cadenas de las Américas* — a real national
broadcasting platform — and used it deliberately to bring African-American poets, Langston
Hughes among them, to Hispanic audiences: cross-community bridging done live on air, not
incidental overlap. Performed directly alongside Hughes and Marian Anderson. A 1939 Howard
University professor's review noted her performance moved an audience that didn't
understand Spanish at all.

- **Honest caveat:** no documented named-protégé credential — her legacy is
  pioneering-and-bridging rather than formally training successors.
- **Conduct caveats:** none. Deceased 1976.

### Arturo Alfonso Schomburg (1874–1938)

Diaspora-wide historical reclamation, opening the Latin/Hispanic-Caribbean and
wider-diaspora thread on this programme.

Self-described "Afroborinqueño" (Black Puerto Rican), born in Santurce to a freeborn
Afro-Caribbean midwife from St. Croix and a German-descended Puerto Rican merchant. A
childhood teacher's claim that Black people had "no history, no heroes, no
accomplishments" drove him to personally amass a 10,000-item collection spanning the whole
African diaspora — deliberately not narrowed to one lane, since the point was showing the
diaspora's breadth and interconnection. That collection became the founding core of the
Schomburg Center for Research in Black Culture (NYPL, Harlem), still operating today,
named after him. Real mentorship credential: once made curator there in 1932, reached out
to and directly guided young Harlem Renaissance scholars, Zora Neale Hurston among them;
his 1925 essay "The Negro Digs Up His Past" became a founding manifesto of the Harlem
Renaissance.

- **Conduct caveats:** none. Deceased 1938.

## TECHreneurs

**Added here (21 Aug 2026, co-tutor alongside Madam C.J. Walker, not replacing her):**

### Marcus Garvey (1887–1940)

Diaspora-wide institution-building at the largest scale on this roster.

Jamaican-born, built the Universal Negro Improvement Association (UNIA) into the largest
Black organisation in history; founded the Black Star Line (1919, the first Black-owned
shipping line, explicitly built to connect Black commerce across the diaspora, with real
offices as far as Costa Rica), the Negro Factories Corporation, and the widely distributed
*Negro World* newspaper.

- **Honest caveat (included per CJ's standing instruction that contribution, not
  sainthood, is the bar):** his 1923 US mail fraud conviction is now well-documented as
  political persecution rather than misconduct — J. Edgar Hoover ran a years-long
  infiltration/sabotage campaign against the UNIA specifically, and President Biden
  formally pardoned him in January 2025, with 20+ members of Congress citing
  "prosecutorial and governmental misconduct."
- **Separate, differently-shaped caveat** (unlike the roster's harm-based exclusions —
  Perry/Brown/Berry/Bambaataa, all dropped for documented harm to specific real people, per
  the source material — this is not that): multiple sources document Garvey drew support
  from and met with white segregationist groups who found ideological common ground in his
  separatist "Back to Africa" philosophy. A real association and a controversy of
  ideas/alliances, not victimisation of anyone in his orbit — treated accordingly, not as
  disqualifying, per the source material's own framing.
- Deceased 1940; remains later moved to Jamaica, where he became the country's first
  National Hero.

### Abdias do Nascimento (1914–2011)

The real teaching/institution-building credential that (per the source material) Holder's
own entry flags as missing — opens the Afro-Brazilian thread.

Founded Brazil's Teatro Experimental do Negro (TEN) in 1944, explicitly to break the
practice of white actors performing Black roles in blackface on the Brazilian stage. TEN
became a real, documented training and launching platform for major Afro-Brazilian
actors — Ruth de Souza and Léa Garcia named among those it launched. Real
institution-building beyond theatre: founded the Museu de Arte Negra (1950), published the
newspaper *Quilombo* (1948–52), ran literacy courses reaching some 800 people, and later
became the first Afro-Brazilian federal legislator to propose affirmative action
legislation in Brazil. Brazil holds the single largest African diaspora population in the
Americas and had no thread on this roster before this entry.

- **Conduct caveats:** none. Deceased 2011.

## Trubble n Bass — new genre: Son/Salsa (genre 20)

### Arsenio Rodríguez (1911–1970) + Johnny Pacheco (1935–2021) — LOCKED

First genre anchoring Latin/Hispanic-Caribbean diasporic music on the roster.

- **Rodríguez** — musical architecture. Blind from age 7, Cuban tres player of Kongo
  descent, developed son montuno and the conjunto format in the 1940s — the actual
  structural template son montuno/mambo/salsa are all built on. When he moved to New York
  in 1952 he left his conjunto to trumpeter Félix Chappottín, who carried it forward; Fania
  All-Star Larry Harlow studied under his direct influence and took the homage nickname "El
  Judío Maravilloso." **Honest caveat:** teaching credential is thinner than most locked
  entries — succession and reverent influence, not documented formal mentorship. Deceased
  1970, no conduct caveats.
- **Pacheco** — institution-building/talent development. Dominican-born, moved to New York
  age 11, studied percussion at Juilliard; co-founded Fania Records in 1963/64, which
  became known as "the Motown of salsa"; coined and popularised the term "salsa" itself; as
  music director/arranger/producer directly fueled the careers of Celia Cruz, Rubén Blades,
  and Héctor Lavoe. Deceased 2021, no conduct caveats.

**Note (from the source material, preserved as instructed):** merengue, bachata, timba,
reggaeton, and samba remain separate open genre slots — not covered by this entry, each
needs its own research pass the same way this one got, not folded in for convenience.
