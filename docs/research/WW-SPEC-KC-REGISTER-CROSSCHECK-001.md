## Question to resolve

Are Pettigrew's "Register of British Slave Traders" (BBC, Sep 2026,
~13,000 names, source of the South Sea Company branding-iron evidence)
and "The City of London and the Atlantic Slave Economy, 1640–1807" (a
leaked ~280-name report, national database due Sep 2026) the same
research output, two outputs of the same wider initiative, or genuinely
separate projects?

## Determination: same wider initiative, genuinely different outputs — and the spec's own framing conflated two of them

Verified via web search against institutional and near-primary sources
(full citation list below). Three distinct Pettigrew-authored/co-authored
outputs exist, not two:

**A. The Register of British Slave Traders** (public name; the AHRC grant's
formal title is "The Trans-Atlantic Slave Trade and the Development of
Europe," TASTADE). PI: Prof William Pettigrew (King's College London).
Co-investigators: Dr Nicholas Radburn (Lancaster), Prof Edmond Smith
(Manchester), Dr Nicholas Draper (UCL). Funder: AHRC (~£1m). Started 2021;
~13,000 individuals/institutions, 1563–1807 (an early 2021 grant
announcement cited ~6,500 — the figure grew as the archival work
completed, not a scale mismatch that rules out identity). **Publicly
launched 14 September 2026** at britishslavetraders.org — confirmed via
King's College London's own news page (primary/institutional). Companion
outputs: Pettigrew's book *Britain's Slave Traders* (Penguin) and a
two-part BBC documentary, *Finding Britain's Slave Traders* (David
Olusoga). The South Sea Company branding-iron evidence (Feb 1714
director-to-agent letter, found by Radburn in the British Library
archives) belongs to **this** project — confirmed via multiple outlets
reporting Radburn's find directly in connection with the Register's BBC
coverage. **This means the spec item 1 attribution already logged is
correct — no correction needed to the South Sea Company entry's
provenance line.**

**B. "Assessing the Connections between the Transatlantic Traffic in
Enslaved African People and the City of London Corporation."** A separate,
smaller commission (£34,400) from the City of London Corporation directly
to Pettigrew (same person, different funding line, not part of the AHRC
grant) — confirmed via the City of London Corporation's own press release
(primary/institutional) announcing the funding in December 2024. Scope:
Lord Mayors, Sheriffs, Aldermen, and Common Councillors who served
1640–1807. This produced the **leaked ~280-name report** (dated 18 May
2026, per the leaked document itself as reported), covered by BBC
(secondary-scholarly-adjacent news reporting, corroborated across
multiple outlets: Yahoo/AOL syndication of the same wire report, Africa
Briefing, RECLAIM EC1). The Corporation told the BBC it was "disappointed
the document had leaked" and planned to publish the completed version
"later in 2026" — **not yet formally published as of this check; only the
leaked draft exists publicly.** **This is what the spec's item 2 actually
refers to** — the ~280-name report and the 1640–1807 date range match
exactly.

**C. A third report, not previously logged anywhere in this spec or the
prior chat-memory framing: "Report assessing the connections between the
transatlantic traffic in enslaved African people and the Bridge House
Estates."** Same author (Pettigrew), commissioned separately by the **City
Bridge Foundation** (the charity historically named Bridge House Estates,
linked to but legally distinct from the City of London Corporation).
**Confirmed already published** — read directly (primary source, PDF
fetched from citybridgefoundation.org.uk/assets/documents/): foreword
signed "Paul Martinelli, Chair, City Bridge Foundation Board, November
2025." The City of London Corporation's own press release explicitly
describes its own commission (B) as complementing "a related study
commissioned by the City Bridge Foundation" (C) — i.e. the Corporation
itself treats B and C as two separate, parallel commissions, not one.

**Where the spec's own framing was wrong, flagged rather than silently
corrected:** the spec described item 2 as *"a three-year study... [that]
produced a leaked ~280-name report; a national searchable database...
scheduled for public release September 2026"* — as if one single named
project produced both the leaked report and the national database. That
conflates B (the City-of-London-specific commission, which produced the
280-name leak) with A (the AHRC Register project, which produced the
national September 2026 database). No source found anywhere — primary or
secondary — names a single project called "The City of London and the
Atlantic Slave Economy, 1640–1807." One secondary aggregator did describe
the Register itself as "a three-year research project" (contradicted by a
different, more directly-sourced figure: KCL's own page places the start
at 2021, i.e. a five-year project to the September 2026 launch, and one
BBC-citing secondary source explicitly calls it "a five-year research
project"); that duration confusion likely fed the original conflation.
This is exactly the class of drift the repo's standing rule exists to
catch: a plausible-sounding chat-memory claim that doesn't survive being
checked against the actual sources.

## Effect on the KC archive's research pause

**The pause can be lifted.** The KC slavery-elites archive was paused
pending the "national searchable database's September 2026 release, so
findings can be built on verifiable named data rather than the leaked
~280-name aggregate." That release is Project A, the Register of British
Slave Traders, and it launched publicly 14 September 2026 — two days
before this check — confirmed live and public at britishslavetraders.org
per KCL's own announcement. Resuming substantive research is explicitly
out of scope for this spec (see "Out of scope" below); this only confirms
the condition that was blocking it has been met.
