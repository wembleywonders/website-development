## Gaspar Yanga — opened 21 Aug 2026, unresolved

**The figure, confirmed:** around 1570, Gaspar Yanga led a group of
enslaved Africans to freedom in the Veracruz highlands (Mexico) and founded
a self-governing settlement, originally San Lorenzo de los Negros, later
renamed Yanga in his honour. The community survived Spanish attacks and
held out for decades before winning a formal 1618 treaty recognising it as
self-governing — one of the earliest free Black settlements formally
recognised in the Americas. Declared a Mexican national hero in 1871.

**Uncorroborated claim — do not repeat as fact without further sourcing:**
one source (Texas Institute for the Preservation of History and Culture)
describes Yanga as "a rebellious Muslim from Nigeria." No other source
found corroborates this. If this holds up, it would connect Yanga to WW's
separate, also-still-open Black Muslim representation thread — but it
hasn't been verified, and should not be asserted as settled.

**Unresolved fit — which programme, if any:** Yanga's real, documented
credential is founding and defending a self-sufficient settlement for
decades — substantial practical achievement (sustaining a community,
defensible planning, survival against a colonial power), but closer to
community-founding and military leadership than to engineering or
architecture specifically. Locking him into STEMgeneers' open engineering
slot on this evidence alone would repeat the exact Neville/Timbuktu-
scholars mismatch pattern already caught and corrected once this session
(see docs/WW-OUTSTANDING-TASKS.md and CLAUDE.md's "What Wembley Wonders
actually is" section) — an impressive figure forced into the wrong home.
Roots, or a new resistance-history thread, may be a better fit than
STEMgeneers — not yet decided.

**What would close this out:** (1) independent corroboration of the
Muslim-identity claim, or a clear finding that it doesn't hold up; (2) a
decision on which programme, if any, his real credential (settlement
founding/defence) actually belongs to. Neither has been done. Do not mark
this LOCKED or resolved until both are addressed — and if further research
surfaces a genuinely STEM-specific Afro-Mexican figure instead, that's a
separate lead, not a reason to force this one to fit.
