### Confirmed accurate

- The corps founded 1622 by Louis XIII from an existing light-cavalry
  company, tasked with royal escort outside residences.
- Cardinal Richelieu's rival/junior musketeer company, created shortly after,
  passing to Mazarin at Richelieu's 1642 death, disbanded by Mazarin in 1646,
  and revived in 1657.
- **d'Artagnan** (real name Charles de Batz de Castelmore, c.1611–1673, from
  Gascony) as a real historical captain-lieutenant of the Musketeers, who
  died at the siege of Maastricht in the Franco-Dutch War on 25 June 1673.
  His fictionalisation stems from Gatien de Courtilz de Sandras's 1700
  semi-fictional *Mémoires de M. d'Artagnan*, published 27 years after the
  real d'Artagnan's death.
- **Dumas's discovery/borrowing** of that book from the Marseille public
  library — a well-corroborated anecdote across multiple sources, including
  the library's own surviving card-index record. **Worth flagging as an
  unresolved wrinkle if reused:** this sits alongside Dumas's own public
  foreword claim of having found manuscripts in the Bibliothèque Nationale
  instead — the two accounts of the discovery aren't reconciled by the source
  material, and this entry doesn't attempt to adjudicate between them either.
- The real, historically attested identities of **Athos** (Armand de Sillègue
  d'Athos d'Autevielle), **Porthos** (Isaac de Porthau), and **Aramis** (Henri
  d'Aramitz) — all genuine members of the Musketeers company contemporaneous
  with d'Artagnan.
- d'Artagnan's role as **Nicolas Fouquet's** arresting officer at Nantes in
  September 1661, and as his escorting jailer to Vincennes and later
  Pignerol.
- The real Musketeers motto, **"Quo ruit et letum"** ("where it rushes, there
  is death"), versus Dumas's invented "all for one, one for all."
- The **Comte de Tréville** (Jean-Armand du Peyrer) as the real historical
  basis for Dumas's M. de Tréville, a genuine captain-lieutenant of the
  Musketeers under Louis XIII. The source transcript garbles this name as
  "the count of Til"/"Til" — confirmed as an audio-to-text transcription
  artifact, not an error in the source video's actual claims, and not
  reproduced as "Til" anywhere in this entry.

### Two minor date discrepancies flagged (neither changes the substantive argument)

1. The corps' 18th-century disbandment is dated by most sources to
   **1775/1776** rather than a single clean "1775," and Louis XVI's
   decree/effective date varies slightly across sources.
2. The corps' final, definitive disbandment is most commonly dated to
   **1 January 1816** rather than the transcript's stated "1815" — likely
   reflecting the decree being signed/dated in late 1815 with the
   disbandment itself effective the following January.
