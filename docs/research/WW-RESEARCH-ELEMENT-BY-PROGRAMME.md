# WW Research Element — By Programme

Companion to the "What Wembley Wonders actually is" section in CLAUDE.md.
That section establishes WHY WW does real research (rare for a CIC, and the
reason academics will take this platform seriously). This document answers
WHAT that research actually investigates, programme by programme: for each
community/tradition a programme touches, what survived the disruptions of
slavery, colonisation, and displacement, what was lost or suppressed, and
how survival actually happened — not as a flattened single diaspora story,
but community by community, with the specific mechanism named.

This is not a content brief. It's the research lens Claude Code should
carry into any work on a given programme — curator research, content gaps,
or investigation tasks — so the "what died, what survived, how" question
stays live rather than being answered once and forgotten.

Status per programme is marked honestly: **DEMONSTRATED** (real research
already done this way, in the actual locked curator entries) vs.
**PROPOSED** (a reasonable research lens, not yet evidenced by actual
work — don't present as settled).

## DEMONSTRATED — the question already drives real, locked research

**Trubble n Bass** — musical structure as survival record. Son/Salsa
(Rodríguez/Pacheco, locked 21 Aug 2026) is a direct case study: what
survived the Middle Passage and Spanish colonisation into Cuban son
montuno's structure, how it then travelled and recombined into salsa in
New York specifically through Puerto Rican and Dominican contact. The
research question here is literal and structural — which rhythmic and
ensemble elements persisted, which were reshaped, and through which named
person the reshaping happened.

**Roots** — the "what survived" question applied to the archival record
itself. Walker's entry is about reconstructing lineage from military/
pension records built by the system that enslaved people, not records kept
for them. Schomburg's entry is about a man who personally went looking for
a record that had been actively denied ("no history, no heroes") and built
the institution that still holds it. This programme's research element
isn't a metaphor — it's the actual mechanics of archival survival and loss.

**Pageturners** — oral vs. written transmission as two different survival
mechanisms. Fasséké represents a tradition that survived because it never
depended on paper (griot lineage, memorised and performed).
Baghayogho/Ahmad Baba represent a written tradition that survived
physically (Sankore's manuscripts, tens of thousands still extant) but was
nearly erased from the story of who produced serious scholarship. Locking
both together was itself a research finding: two different survival
mechanisms for the same underlying loss (denial that the diaspora produced
serious intellectual work).

**Kaywana's Court** — what happens to oral performance tradition under
exile specifically, not just displacement. The Garifuna were expelled from
Saint Vincent in 1797 — an actively engineered separation from land, not a
gradual migration — and Nabor/Palacio's entry is about what performance
tradition (paranda, then the Garifuna Collective) looked like surviving
that specific kind of rupture, and how it nearly disappeared before
Palacio's 2007 album carried it to a global audience for the first time.

**TECHreneurs** — what economic self-sufficiency looked like when built
deliberately outside, and in spite of, systems designed to exclude.
Walker's is domestic-scale (a training/franchise system). Garvey's is the
largest-scale attempt on this whole roster (a shipping line, a
manufacturing corporation, explicitly built to connect Black commerce
across the diaspora rather than through it). Locking them together is a
real research pairing: same underlying question, two radically different
scales of attempted answer.

**Easy Street** — what it took to break a specific, actively maintained
mechanism of erasure (white actors in blackface performing Black
characters) in three different national contexts at once — US radio/TV
(Durham), Trinidad/Broadway (Holder), and Brazil (Nascimento, founding TEN
explicitly in response to watching a white actor in blackface perform
Emperor Jones). Same mechanism of harm, three independently-arrived-at
responses. That's a real comparative finding, not three unrelated
biographies.

**Rayd-yo** — broadcasting specifically as the medium that could cross
language barriers print couldn't. Williams' entry is about
institution-building and editorial gatekeeping within one language
community. Cosme's is about using the same medium deliberately to carry
work (Langston Hughes' poetry) across the English/Spanish divide live, in
the 1940s. The research question: what could radio do for cross-community
survival of culture that print structurally couldn't.

## OPEN — a real thread mid-investigation, not yet resolved

**STEMgeneers** — the "what survived" question here is still genuinely
unanswered, not just under-researched. The Baghayogho/Ahmad Baba lead
turned out to belong to Pageturners instead (legal/theological scholarship,
not STEM). The Gaspar Yanga lead (Afro-Mexican settlement-founding and
defensive engineering, possibly connected to a Black Muslim identity claim
that's still only single-sourced) is open and unresolved as of 21 Aug 2026
— see docs/WW-OUTSTANDING-TASKS.md and the research-vessel-principle
notes. This is the clearest current example of the "genuinely unclear is a
legitimate answer" principle in practice: don't force a STEM figure into
this slot to close the gap.

## PROPOSED — a reasonable lens, not yet evidenced by real work

**Auntie Anansi's Kitchen** — food as a survival mechanism operating under
conditions specifically designed to erase identity. The Baianas do acarajé
lead (Afro-Brazilian women, Candomblé-linked, IPHAN-protected intangible
heritage) fits this exactly but was deliberately NOT forced into the
named-curator format the rest of the roster uses — it's a living communal
practice, not a single documentable figure. How it actually gets presented
on the platform is still undecided.

**Silk Stilettos, Scrap Cat, G-Tech Casters, Joystick, Bright Sparks** — no
research work has been done connecting these programmes to the
what-died/what-survived question yet. Don't assume a lens for these that
hasn't actually been investigated — that would be exactly the failure mode
(forcing a plausible-sounding fit) this whole discipline exists to catch.
If asked to do curator or content research on any of these, that research
starts from nothing, not from an assumed frame.

## Standing instruction for Claude Code

When working on ANY programme's content or curator research, ask what this
programme's real "what died, what survived, how" question is before
treating a figure or a fact as just interesting or diverse. A figure earns
a place because their real, documented life demonstrates part of that
mechanism for a real community — not because they add representation in
the abstract. This is the same discipline that already caught Neville
(real, but not a naming-diversity case), Calderón and Landers (real, but
living or the wrong person), and the Gaspar Yanga thread (real, but not
yet resolved) — apply it forward, not just backward at what's already
locked.
